# Scandal Makes Jensen Huang's Nvidia Exit Likely

*Information cutoff: about 00:30–00:40 UTC on November 12, 2026 (about 7:30 PM ET / 4:30 PM PT on November 11). As of then, Nvidia had announced no board decision and made no new statement on Huang's status, and researchers found no 8-K. All market prices are a snapshot from the Kalshi and Polymarket APIs at that time and will have moved. The November 11 contracts settled a few hours after the snapshot.*

Jensen Huang will probably not be Nvidia's CEO for much longer. I put the chance that his exit is **announced by December 31, 2026 at about 80%**, and about 75% that he has actually left the job by then. I put it at **about 85% within one year, 90% within three years and 92% within five years**. Before the scandal, the baseline was roughly 5%, 15% and 28%. The jump follows a pattern that has ended almost every comparable CEO tenure. On November 8 he admitted an extramarital affair, reportedly with a Megaspeed executive whom US intelligence links to Chinese state security and chip diversion. On November 11 the NYT reported a second relationship, this one with a direct report. Within 48 hours of its first statement the board dropped its "confidence in Jensen" language, the WSJ and CBS reported that directors are preparing to oust him, and Bloomberg reported that they are working with an outside firm on a successor. Prediction markets are more certain than I am: **Kalshi 88/89¢ and Polymarket 90/91¢ for a year-end exit**. I think they are modestly overpriced, by roughly 8–10 points at year-end and 4–6 points on long-dated contracts. They give too little weight to two outcomes that resolve "No" on both platforms: Huang stepping aside while the investigation runs, or surviving with concessions. On hour-by-hour timing, though, the markets see live reporting and deserve deference. There is **no true arbitrage between the two platforms**. The visible 4¢ gap (Kalshi YES at 64¢ plus Polymarket NO at 32¢) is really a bet that Nvidia will not announce a *future* departure. Polymarket pays when a departure is announced, while Kalshi pays only once he actually leaves. The spread prices that scenario at about 4–7% for the November 12–13 deadlines, close to my own estimate. Every decisive fact after November 8 rests on anonymous sources. None of this is investment advice.

These numbers come from the scenario weights in the final section. I give **76% to a formal exit decided in the coming weeks**, made up of three paths: an immediate ouster or resignation with an interim CEO (54%), a negotiated move into a non-CEO role effective at once (15%), or an announced future step-down while a successor is found (7%). I give **10% to Huang stepping aside on leave** while the newly hired investigators work. The remaining **14% is survival**: 9% with a reprimand and governance concessions, 5% with substantial exoneration. Even a surviving Huang would face elevated risk from the DOJ probe, Congress and any further allegations. That risk turns about two-thirds of the step-aside path and a third of the reprimand path into departures within a year. After the first year, leftover scandal risk and the ordinary retirement risk around age 65 add only a few points, because most of the probability is used up in the next few months.

Three things make the markets look overpriced to me. First, the step-aside path is real, and both contracts exclude it. The independent directors hired former acting SDNY US Attorney Joon Kim and Wachtell, Lipton only on November 10 ([Nvidia Newsroom](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-the-independent-members-of-the-nvidia-board)). Boards often keep an accused CEO in place, or on leave, until investigators report. CBS kept Moonves in place for six weeks ([WTOP/AP](https://wtop.com/national/2018/09/les-moonves-resigns-from-cbs/)), Nestlé ran two probes before firing Freixe ([CFO Dive](https://www.cfodive.com/news/nestle-ceo-firing-followed-second-probe-office-romance-cfo-says/759324/)), and Kalanick took a leave a week before resigning ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/06/20/uber-ceo-travis-kalanick-resigns)). Second, "preparing to oust" is an intention reported by people familiar with the matter, not a vote. The Powell relationship rests on the accounts of five former employees, which Nvidia, Huang and Powell have not confirmed, and the NYT did not obtain the reported video ([Kalshi News](https://news.kalshi.com/p/jensen-huang-exit-odds-spike-80-percent-dec-1-2026); [OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/nvidias-board-working-with-outside-firm-to-replace-jensen-huang-could-name-temporary-chief-bloomberg/)). Third, this board has reasons to flinch. Its average tenure is nearly twice the S&P 500 norm, it has no successor ready, and analysts estimate Nvidia is worth 25–30% less without Huang ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/09/2026/with-a-weak-board-and-no-successor-nvidia-confronts-a-governance-crisis); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/article/nvidia-is-worth-nearly-30-less-if-jensen-huang-is-no-longer-ceo-tech-analyst-114242269.html)). Columbia's John Coffee put it bluntly: "negligence doesn't require that you shoot yourself in the foot by firing a well regarded CEO" ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/09/2026/with-a-weak-board-and-no-successor-nvidia-confronts-a-governance-crisis)).

The case for the markets is also strong. Three outlets reported the board's move independently, and similar leaks have usually come before action: Wynn, Kalanick and Moonves all left within days to weeks of comparable reports. A second wave of allegations involving a subordinate is the strongest single predictor of exit in the precedent set. Markets also see live reporting that these notes do not. The roughly 30-point jump between Polymarket's November 11 and November 12 contracts shows traders expected a decision within about a day, and on timing I defer to them. My disagreement is about where the probability levels off. Contracts priced above my ~80% year-end level look rich: the November 30 contracts at 83–86¢, the year-end contracts at 88–91¢ and the long-dated Kalshi contracts at 94–98¢. The November 12–13 contracts, at 63–74¢, are a pure timing call that I cannot improve on.

The crisis has two layers. The national-security layer came first and was not enough to sink Huang on its own. Alice Huang allegedly used industry contacts and shell companies to access about **240,000 export-banned chips** ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/09/alice-huang-nvidia-jensen-megaspeed)). The Information reported that her network enabled about $7.5B in Nvidia chip sales to China, a figure seen only through a [Yahoo Finance live blog](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/live/nvidia-live-updates-nvidia-stock-tanks-in-after-hours-after-allegations-of-a-second-affair-surface-222438011.html) and not verified. Even so, on November 8 the board said it had "no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised," and Nvidia said Huang would lead "without interruption" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/08/nvidia-jensen-huang-email-board-of-directors-statement.html)). Trump said Huang "had absolutely no idea that the person in question was a spy" ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/corporations/trump-fbi-probe-nvidia-jensen-huang-spy-rcna608439)). When Polymarket's year-end contract opened on November 8 it traded at about **16.5%** ([Lines.com](https://www.lines.com/prediction-markets/business/jensen-huang-announced-resignation-or-removal-by-december-31)), and on November 9 Kalshi put the odds he would be gone before 2027 at 17% ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/jensen-huang-nvidia-exit-prediction-markets.html)).

The conduct layer is what changed the board. Powell, 48, has led Nvidia's healthcare business since 2017. The NYT reported she was seen being affectionate with Huang at a San Jose hotel during GTC in March 2026 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/11/nvidia-jensen-huang-affair-employee.html)), and Bloomberg reported that she reported directly to him ([Bloomingbit/Bloomberg](https://en.bloomingbit.io/feed/news/124240)). A relationship with a direct report is a conduct and disclosure issue. The board can act on it without any finding on national security, and that is why the second report mattered more than the first. Minutes after the NYT story, the WSJ reported that the board was **"preparing to oust"** Huang and could meet as soon as Wednesday, November 11 ([Reuters/Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/nvidia-is-preparing-to-oust-ceo-jensen-huang-wsj-reports-5019991)). CBS cited two sources saying an ouster could come "soon" and that the Trump administration was aware ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/nvidia-board-ceo-jensen-huang-china/)). Bloomberg reported that directors were working with an outside firm to identify replacements and were weighing a temporary CEO ([OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/nvidias-board-working-with-outside-firm-to-replace-jensen-huang-could-name-temporary-chief-bloomberg/)). NVDA traded at **$160.95 at 6:30 PM ET on November 11, down 13.8% from its November 6 close** ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/nvidia-board-reportedly-preparing-oust-233419551.html)). Political cover thinned too. Republican Senator Kevin Cramer, Democratic Representative Suhas Subramanyam and Republican Representative Don Bacon all called for him to resign ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/11/nvidia-jensen-huang-congress-resign-ai-chips.html)), and Baird downgraded the stock, citing uncertainty over Huang's tenure ([Yahoo Finance live blog](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/live/nvidia-scandal-live-updates-nvidia-stock--board-investigates-ceo-jensen-huang-affair-alleged-chinese-spy-163130372.html)).

Readers should keep two kinds of fact apart. On the record are Huang's November 8 email ("I was unfaithful to my wife… I am fully committed to NVIDIA and will work to earn back your confidence"), which names no one ([Nvidia Newsroom](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/a-message-to-our-company)); the board's two statements; the November 9 class action, whose class period runs October 9 to November 8; and public comments from Trump and lawmakers. Everything else rests on anonymous sources: the US intelligence assessment of Alice Huang, the Powell relationship, the WSJ, CBS and Bloomberg accounts of the board, and the DOJ criminal probe of Megaspeed, which NBC sourced to a single person. The Bloomberg report was read here only through secondary summaries. No charges against Jensen or Alice Huang have been reported, and nothing published establishes that he knowingly took part in espionage or unlawful chip transfers ([PBS/AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/nvidia-and-huang-face-lawsuit-alleging-investors-were-misled-about-spy-affair)). Some dates also conflict: CNBC dates the NYT spy story to November 6 rather than October 29 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/jensen-huang-affair-nvidia-succession.html)).

### Five checkpoints should settle it within weeks

The first is the board itself. A change of CEO must be disclosed in an 8-K under Item 5.02, and researchers found no such filing as of the cutoff. The second is the **November 17 earnings call**, where the question is who speaks. Q3 guidance was about $108B after Q2 revenue of $96.2B, up 106% ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/nvidia-operating-income-jumps-124-to-63-7-billion-as-revenue-doubles-and-q3-outlook-hits-108-billion/)), and boards prefer leadership clarity before a call, which helps explain why markets expect a decision this week. The third is the **November 18 House deadline**. House committee chairs Brian Mast and John Moolenaar had not heard back from Huang as of November 11 and raised the possibility of subpoenas ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/11/nvidia-jensen-huang-congress-resign-ai-chips.html)). The fourth is the **DOJ National Security Division and FBI**. Any charge against Alice Huang or Megaspeed executives would sharply raise the stakes; Polymarket priced a federal charge against her by year-end at about 12%, in a thin market ([Polymarket API](https://gamma-api.polymarket.com/public-search?q=jensen%20huang)). The fifth is the Kim/Wachtell findings, especially whether the Powell relationship was disclosed and whether Huang influenced her pay. Beyond those five, watch Powell's own status, any statement from BlackRock (7.43%) or Vanguard (7.31%) ([2026 proxy](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000036/nvda-20260512.htm)), and whether Huang appears in public at all. Kalshi priced 21/29 that he would be seen in public before November 13.

## A board majority can remove a founder who owns 3.6%

Removing Huang as CEO is legally simple. Under Section 29(a) of Nvidia's bylaws, every officer serves "at the pleasure of the Board" and "may be removed at any time by the Board of Directors." Section 22 lets **any two directors call a special meeting on 24 hours' notice** ([Nvidia bylaws](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581024000069/exhibit31-amendedandrestat.htm)). Huang beneficially owns 870.6M shares, **3.58%** of the company, most of it held with his wife Lori in trusts over which they share voting power. Nvidia has no dual-class shares, and Huang is not chairman ([2026 proxy](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000036/nvda-20260512.htm)). Law professor Lipton told TechCrunch that this makes him "easier to dislodge" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/nvidias-crisis-has-corporate-governance-experts-reaching-for-the-popcorn)). Removing him as a *director* is a different matter. That requires a stockholder vote: a majority of outstanding shares with cause, or 66⅔% without ([bylaws](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581024000069/exhibit31-amendedandrestat.htm)). An ousted Huang could therefore keep his board seat unless he resigns it.

The practical constraint is the board's character. Of 11 directors, ten are independent. There is no chair; instead there is a lead independent director, Stephen Neal, 77, chairman emeritus of the law firm Cooley. Neal drew the highest vote against any director at the June 2026 annual meeting, about 13.3% ([8-K, June 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000056/nvda-20260624.htm)). Tench Coxe and Harvey Jones have been directors since 1993, and Coxe and Mark Stevens come from Sutter Hill and Sequoia, Nvidia's early venture backers. Semafor puts average tenure at "almost 15 years, nearly twice the S&P 500 average," and notes that the board has no current or former S&P 500 CEO and no national-security experience. Penn's Jill Fisch said the composition "suggests this is… a CEO exercising effective control over the board instead of vice versa" ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/09/2026/with-a-weak-board-and-no-successor-nvidia-confronts-a-governance-crisis)). The flip side is that these directors own large stakes and are watching them shrink.

The grounds for removal are narrower than at Intel or McDonald's, which had explicit fraternization bans. Nvidia's public Code of Conduct contains no such ban. It lists "personal relationships" among activities that "may create a conflict of interest," and it requires employees to remove themselves from related decisions. Any waiver for an executive officer needs written approval from the board's Compliance Committee and must be disclosed ([Code of Conduct](https://www.nvidia.com/content/dam/en-zz/Solutions/about-us/documents/NVIDIA-Code-of-Conduct-external.pdf)). The case therefore turns on non-disclosure, on whether Huang influenced Powell's pay or advancement, and on whether he was candid with the board. Lipton frames three questions: was the law broken, was policy violated, and was the board misled. Professor Henderson adds that if Huang knew Alice Huang was a suspected spy and did not tell the board, that would be "a fireable offense" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/nvidias-crisis-has-corporate-governance-experts-reaching-for-the-popcorn)).

The board has no financial carrots or sticks. Huang's FY2026 pay was $36.3M, and his unvested awards were worth about **$265M**. His stake, by contrast, is worth roughly $140B at the November 11 price (870.6M shares × $160.95, my arithmetic) ([2026 proxy](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000036/nvda-20260512.htm)), so forfeiture "for cause" would barely matter to him. Nvidia has never disclosed a CEO succession plan. The proxy's "Senior Management Development" section has the CEO himself choosing which leaders engage with the board ([2026 proxy](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000036/nvda-20260512.htm)). Huang has said, "I'm famous in saying that I don't believe in succession planning" ([Lex Fridman transcript](https://lexfridman.com/jensen-huang-transcript)). Nvidia has no president or COO, and Huang had 33 direct reports in the week of November 9 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/jensen-huang-affair-nvidia-succession.html)).

Governance experts, not board leaks, have named the interim candidates: CFO **Colette Kress**, 58 and CFO since 2013, and lead director Neal ([OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/nvidias-board-working-with-outside-firm-to-replace-jensen-huang-could-name-temporary-chief-bloomberg/); [FY2026 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm)). Names floated for the permanent job include outsiders Lisa Su, Hock Tan, Rene Haas and Pat Gelsinger, and insiders such as Ian Buck ([Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/who-could-replace-jensen-huang-as-nvidia-ceo-nobody-12695728)). Georgetown's Jason Schloetzer argues that "a horse race with 60 horses has no good solution," which points toward an outside hire ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/09/2026/with-a-weak-board-and-no-successor-nvidia-confronts-a-governance-crisis)). Huang's children Madison and Spencer hold director-level jobs, his son-in-law Nico Caprez is a VP, and Huang said in October that "they have to go earn it" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/09/what-is-nvidia-ceo-succession-plan-jensen-huang-son-in-law-nico-caprez/)). Huang's own trading also gives no hint that he expected to leave. His last open-market sales were on October 29, 2025 ([Form 4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1197649/000119764925000056/xslF345X05/wk-form4_1761944802.xml)). No new pre-arranged (10b5-1) selling plan has been disclosed since, and his 2026 filings show only gifts, tax withholding and estate-planning transfers ([Form 4, Sept 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1197649/000119764926000012/xslF345X06/wk-form4_1789765856.xml)).

The research team compiled nine high-profile cases in which a hired CEO faced a board investigation or press exposure over an undisclosed relationship, a relationship-linked conflict or other misconduct: the eight hired CEOs in the table plus Astronomer's Andy Byron, who resigned about three days after a viral video ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/andy-byron-astronomer-board-investigation-coldplay-kiss-cam-video)). Every one of the nine left. Most left within days to six weeks. Strong results protected no one. Easterbrook was fired while McDonald's shares had roughly doubled under him, and the board said the decision was unrelated to performance ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2019-11-03/mcdonald-s-fires-ceo-easterbrook-over-relationship-with-employee)). Two features recur in the cases that ended fastest. One is a second wave of allegations (Moonves, Looney, Freixe). The other is evidence that the CEO was not candid with the board (Hurd, Looney, Freixe). Nvidia already has the first. The Kim/Wachtell investigation will decide the second.

Founders get only partial protection. Wynn left 11 days after the WSJ report even while denying it, and Kalanick resigned a week after taking leave once five major investors demanded it. The founders who survived either controlled the vote or had an overwhelming revolt behind them. Altman was reinstated after about 500 of roughly 700 OpenAI employees threatened to quit, and the SpaceX board took no formal action against Musk. Huang has no voting control. His revolt is also thinner: a Blind survey found that 88% of 232 Nvidia employees want him to stay ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/nvidia-board-reportedly-preparing-oust-233419551.html)), a small sample compared with OpenAI's mass petition.

The precedent list is biased toward exits, because probes that end quietly rarely become public. In a broader academic sample of executive personal indiscretions, **most accused executives kept their jobs**, with turnover of about 25% for first offenders ([Cline, Walkling & Yore, JFE 2018](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:127:y:2018:i:2:p:389-415); [AEA version](https://topcat.aeaweb.org/conference/2014/retrieve.php?pdfid=797)). On the other side, ethical lapses caused 39% of forced CEO exits at the world's 2,500 largest companies in 2018 ([CNBC on Strategy&](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/05/15/pwc-strategy-report-top-reason-ceos-were-ousted-in-2018-was-scandals.html)). My reading is that the nine-of-nine rate applies once facts are confirmed and public. The Powell facts are not yet confirmed, which is why I keep 14% on survival.

The form of exit matters as much as the fact of it, because it decides how the two markets resolve. In the misconduct precedents, departures were **effective immediately**, often without severance, and none of the CEOs kept an executive-chair role. Founders sometimes kept a board seat or a non-executive chair title (Kalanick, Neumann). Interim leadership usually went to the CFO or the board chair. The only no-successor case, Intel, produced the worst shareholder result in a Yahoo Finance comparison: a one-year total return of −8.7% against +8.5% for the S&P 500 ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/nvidia-board-reportedly-preparing-oust-233419551.html)). This pattern argues against the deferred-departure scenario that splits Kalshi from Polymarket.

## Before October, Huang's exit risk was about 5% a year

S&P 500 CEO turnover rose from about 10% in 2024 to 12–13% in 2025. Spencer Stuart counted 59 new S&P 500 CEOs in 2025. Departing S&P 500 CEOs averaged **62.9 years old** and about 8.5 years in the job, or 11.3 years in tech. Among 2025 transitions, 23% kept the outgoing CEO on as executive chair ([Spencer Stuart](https://www.spencerstuart.com/-/media/2026/02/ceo-transitions-us/2025-s-p-1500-ceo-transitions-behind-the-ceo-moment.pdf)). Turnover jumps at age 65 and stays higher afterward ([Jenter & Lewellen, NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w17663.pdf)). By my arithmetic, a constant 10–12% annual hazard implies 27–32% over three years and 41–47% over five.

Huang departs from that average in ways that mostly lower his risk. Founder-CEOs are less likely than outsiders to be fired for poor performance ([Kowalewski](https://www.ieseg.fr/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/2019-ACF-01_Kowalewski.pdf)), and Nvidia's performance was at a record, with revenue up 106% year over year. Huang had personally committed to a product roadmap running through Rubin Ultra in 2027 and Feynman in 2028 ([HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/2026/03/17/huang-shares-nvidia-roadmap-showing-more-chips-nvl1152-scale-up-cpo/)). His own statements left no ambiguity. In 2023 he said he would stay "three or four decades" ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/nvidia-ceo-says-he-can-run-company-as-a-robot-for-another-30-years/)). In January 2026 he said he would stay "for as long as I deserve it," adding that the secret to a long tenure is "don't get fired, and, two, don't get bored" ([Yahoo/Quartz](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/long-jensen-huang-nvidias-ceo-132923787.html)). In March 2026 he said he hoped "I die on the job" ([Lex Fridman transcript](https://lexfridman.com/jensen-huang-transcript)). Other founders who were still CEO at 63 stayed well beyond 68: Larry Ellison handed over at 70 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2014/09/18/larry-ellison-to-step-down-as-ceo-of-oracle/)), Morris Chang first stepped down at about 74 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Morris-Chang)), and Hock Tan committed to stay "through 2030, at least" ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-09/broadcom-ceo-poised-for-payout-if-he-reaches-2030-ai-sales-goal)).

Health is a small factor. Using US life tables, an average 63-year-old man has about a **1.6% chance of dying within a year, 5% within three and 9% within five** ([CDC life table](https://ftp.cdc.gov/pub/Health_Statistics/NCHS/Publications/NVSR/74-02/Table02.xlsx)). Men in the top 1% of income live about 14.6 years longer than those in the bottom 1% ([Fortune on Chetty et al.](https://fortune.com/2016/04/11/one-percent-health-decade/)), so halving those figures (my assumption) gives about 0.8%, 2.6% and 4.6%. No health problems have been reported.

The research team's generic estimate for a founder-CEO with Huang's profile was about 7%, 22% and 38% over one, three and five years. I lower that to **about 5%, 15% and 28%** because of his stated intent, the roadmap he is tied to and Nvidia's performance. I keep some upward pressure from age: he turns 65 in February 2028, and Tim Cook's 2026 handover at 65 is a recent example of the norm ([ABC7](https://abc7news.com/post/apple-ceo-tim-cook-is-stepping-down-john-ternus-set-replace/18928305/)). The irony is plain. Of the two risks Huang himself named, "don't get fired" is the one that is now live.

## Kalshi and Polymarket price different events, so the spread is a bet

### The snapshot shows a spread that shrinks as deadlines lengthen

| Deadline (end of day) | Kalshi YES bid/ask | Polymarket YES bid/ask | Kalshi YES + Poly NO | Kalshi NO + Poly YES | Implied odds of "announced, not yet effective" |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nov 11 | 24/25 | 38/39 | 87¢ | 115¢ | 13–15% |
| Nov 12 | 63/64 | 68/69 | 96¢ | 106¢ | 4–6% |
| Nov 13 | 67/68 | 72/74 | 96¢ | 107¢ | 4–7% |
| Nov 30 | 83/84 | 85/86 | 99¢ | 103¢ | 1–3% |
| Dec 31 | 88/89 | 90/91 | 99¢ | 103¢ | 1–3% |

Prices are from the coordinator's pull of the [Kalshi](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?series_ticker=KXCEONVDA&limit=50) and [Polymarket](https://gamma-api.polymarket.com/public-search?q=jensen%20huang) APIs at about 00:30–00:40 UTC on November 12. Kalshi's "before Nov 13" contract corresponds to the end of November 12, and so on. Costs use ask prices and exclude fees. A researcher snapshot taken minutes earlier had the nearest Kalshi contracts 3–4¢ different, which shows how fast these prices move.

| Rule | Kalshi (KXCEONVDA) | Polymarket ("announced resignation or removal by…") |
|---|---|---|
| Pays YES on | Departure announced after the market opened and before the date; "The CEO must actually leave their position; announcing a future departure alone does not qualify" | An announcement that Huang "has resigned or been removed, or will resign or be removed," if intended to take effect within 18 months |
| Move to another Nvidia role | YES once he leaves the CEO post | YES (explicitly counts) |
| Leave or suspension | He would still hold the post, so probably NO | NO (temporary, administrative or indefinite leave excluded) |
| Death or other means | Not addressed in the rule text | NO |
| Settlement source | Bloomberg | Official statements; "a consensus of credible reporting may also be used" |
| Fees | Quadratic, about 0.07 × P × (1−P) per contract (≈1.6¢ at 64¢) | Taker fees enabled |

Polymarket pays YES in every case where Kalshi does, plus some extra ones; the only exceptions are edge cases such as death. Polymarket should therefore trade at or above Kalshi for the same date, and through year-end it does. The gap is effectively a price on "announced but not yet effective" by each deadline. It is about 13–15% for November 11, when a late-evening announcement could easily carry a next-day effective date. It is 4–7% for November 12–13. By late November and year-end it shrinks to 1–3%, because most deferred departures would have taken effect by then.

### The 4-cent "arbitrage" is a short position on a negotiated transition

| Outcome by end of Nov 12 | Kalshi YES 64¢ + Poly NO 32¢ (cost 96¢) | Kalshi NO 37¢ + Poly YES 69¢ (cost 106¢) |
|---|---|---|
| Both YES (exit effective by the deadline) | +4¢ | −6¢ |
| Both NO (no announcement) | +4¢ | −6¢ |
| Polymarket YES, Kalshi NO (future departure announced) | **−96¢** | **+94¢** |
| Kalshi YES, Polymarket NO (death, or a disputed reading of a leave) | +104¢ | −106¢ |
| Break-even probability of the Poly-YES/Kalshi-NO outcome, before fees | ~4% | ~6% |

Fees, size and the tail risk kill the first pair. Kalshi's fee of about 1.6¢, plus Polymarket's taker fee, cuts the 4¢ gross edge to about 1–2¢, which lowers the break-even to roughly 2%. The Polymarket bid that the NO leg must hit showed only about **1,600 contracts at 68¢**. At most, then, the trade is worth roughly $30 of expected net edge against about $1,500 that disappears if Nvidia announces a future departure. The reverse pair is capped by the roughly 450 contracts offered at 69¢ on Polymarket.

The scenario that splits the platforms is a founder-negotiated transition. In it, Huang stays as CEO until a successor is chosen, or is announced as moving to chair at a later date. I weight that at about 7% overall. Applying the market's own timing, where roughly three-quarters of eventual announcements arrive by the end of November 12, gives about 5% for that deadline. That sits inside the priced 4–6% band, so I see neither pair as having positive expected value after fees in the near term. The two sides of the argument roughly cancel. Scandal precedents argue for a lower figure, because immediate effect was the norm. The lack of any successor argues for a higher one: D.A. Davidson's Gil Luria said removing Huang immediately "could be challenging" without a succession plan ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/nvidia-board-reportedly-preparing-oust-233419551.html)).

The year-end pair is the one place where my weights see a sliver of value. I put about 5% on a future departure being announced but not yet effective by December 31, against the 1–3% priced. On that view, Kalshi NO at 12¢ plus Polymarket YES at 91¢ (103¢ total) carries about +2¢ of expected value before fees and roughly zero after Kalshi's ~0.7¢ fee and Polymarket's. That is a thin view trade, not an arbitrage. Manifold's play-money markets show a wider gap, 87.7% for "leave or announce leaving in 2026" against 77% for "cease to be CEO before the end of 2026" ([Manifold](https://manifold.markets/Simon74fe/jensen-huang-leave-or-announce-leav); [Manifold](https://manifold.markets/cash/will-jensen-huang-cease-to-be-ceo-o-EAztPCE5CC)). The wording differs and volumes are tiny, but it suggests some traders see more deferral risk than the real-money spread reflects.

One more ambiguity favors the first pair. If Nvidia puts Huang on leave and names an interim CEO, Polymarket resolves NO by rule. Kalshi's "actually leave their position" should also mean NO, but a Bloomberg headline saying he "stepped aside" could invite a dispute.

### The long end does not add up

At the longer dates the ordering flips. Kalshi's "before Jan 1, 2028" contract at 94/95 traded above Polymarket's "by Dec 31, 2027" at 92.5 (mid), even though Kalshi's contract pays in fewer cases. Exploiting the gap (Polymarket YES plus Kalshi NO, about 99¢) would earn about 1¢ over 13 months. It would lose almost everything if Huang dies in office or leaves by some route Polymarket excludes, so it is not worth doing, but it is a sign that the long Kalshi contracts are rich. A bigger inconsistency sits elsewhere. Polymarket's "Next CEO of NVIDIA?" market resolves to **"Jensen Huang (No Change)"** if no permanent successor is announced by December 31, 2028 ([Polymarket](https://polymarket.com/event/next-ceo-of-nvidia)), and that outcome traded at **18/20**. Meanwhile Kalshi priced Huang's departure before January 1, 2029 at **96/97**. Both prices could hold only if Nvidia had a ~15% chance of running on an interim CEO for two years. I estimate "No Change" at about 13%: roughly 11% that he is still CEO at the end of 2028, plus about 2% for an extended interim. On that view, both prices are stretched in opposite directions.

### Successor and side markets lean toward insiders and drama

Kalshi's next-CEO market ([KXNVDACEO](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?series_ticker=KXNVDACEO)) and Polymarket's have closely matching rules: a permanent successor announced by the end of 2028, with interim CEOs excluded. In the coordinator's snapshot, Ian Buck was 24/39 on Kalshi and 30/31 on Polymarket, Colette Kress 19/28 and 21/21.5, and Jay Puri 12/20 and 8/10. Only one quote is crossed: Puri, where Kalshi's 12¢ bid sits above Polymarket's 10¢ ask. That 2¢ gap covers only tens of contracts on Kalshi and is absorbed by fees and up to two years of locked-up capital.

On substance, the market's lean toward insiders (Buck, Kress and Puri together near 60%, which leaves about 20% for everyone else) sits awkwardly with the board's use of an outside search firm. It also conflicts with Semafor's reporting that directors would likely look outside, to Su or Tan ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/09/2026/with-a-weak-board-and-no-successor-nvidia-confronts-a-governance-crisis)). Puri, 71, announced his retirement as head of worldwide field operations in June, and Nick Parker replaced him in August ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000060/nvda-20260628.htm)), so he looks rich at 8–20%. Kress is cast mainly as an interim "stabilizer" ([Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/who-could-replace-jensen-huang-as-nvidia-ceo-nobody-12695728)), and interim appointments do not count. Kalshi's "next permanent CEO announced before Jan 1, 2027" traded at 46/55 in an illiquid market. I would put it nearer 30%. A search that began around November 11 rarely concludes in seven weeks, and McDonald's named a successor the same day only because it had a ready insider.

Among the side markets, Polymarket's "Huang arrested by Dec 31" at 17/21 (thin) looks rich: no charges have been reported, and the reported DOJ probe targets Megaspeed. "Huang resigns from the Nvidia board by Nov 30" at 52/54 implies about a 60% chance that a CEO exit by then also takes him off the board, and the precedents are split on that. "Divorce by Dec 31" at 22/23 matters more than it looks, because hundreds of millions of his shares sit in trusts where Lori Huang shares voting power ([2026 proxy](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000036/nvda-20260512.htm)). "Kimberly Powell resignation or removal by Dec 31" traded at 62/70.

The stock market offers a rough cross-check. NVDA fell 13.8% from its November 6 close while year-end exit odds rose about 74 points. That implies the market values Huang at roughly 19% of Nvidia, below Dan Ives's 25–30%. The decline also reflects China and legal risk, so the attribution is loose.

### Fees, access and settlement eat what's left

Polymarket's international exchange, where these markets are listed, blocks US persons. Polymarket US is a separate app with a narrower list of markets, so US readers should check availability. Kalshi's MCP connector can read markets but cannot place orders. The two platforms also settle from different sources: Kalshi from Bloomberg, Polymarket from official statements plus "a consensus of credible reporting." Readers of the coverage should note that CNBC, which has publicized Kalshi's odds, discloses a commercial relationship with Kalshi and a minority investment in it ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/jensen-huang-nvidia-exit-prediction-markets.html)). None of this is investment advice. Any position here is a view on a fast-moving news event with asymmetric resolution risk.

## Six endings, from overnight ouster to exoneration

| Scenario | Weight | Precedent | Kalshi | Polymarket | Out within 1 yr |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Immediate ouster or resignation; interim CEO (Kress or Neal) and outside search | 54% | Krzanich, Looney, Freixe | YES | YES | ~100% |
| 2. Negotiated exit to a non-CEO role (founder, adviser, director or chair), effective now | 15% | Neumann, Kalanick (kept board seats) | YES | YES | ~100% |
| 3. Announced future step-down; Huang stays until a successor is named | 7% | Bezos and Cook timetables | NO until effective | YES | ~95% |
| 4. Steps aside on leave pending the Kim/Wachtell probe | 10% | Kalanick's leave | Probably NO | NO | ~65% |
| 5. Survives with reprimand and governance concessions | 9% | Looney's 2022 review | NO | NO | ~30% |
| 6. Probe substantially clears him | 5% | Altman 2023 | NO | NO | ~10% |
| **Weighted** | 100% | | **~75% by Dec 31** | **~80% by Dec 31** | **~85%** |

**Scenario 1, an immediate ouster, is the modal outcome.** A relationship with a direct report has been the most reliable exit trigger, a search firm has been hired, and the board's intent has leaked. The most likely form is an interim CEO, probably Kress, followed by an outside search. Huang would likely remain a director unless he resigns, since removing him from the board takes a stockholder vote. Intel shows the cost of this path: a no-successor transition produced the worst shareholder outcome in the precedent set.

**Scenario 2 is the face-saving version.** Attorney Brian Burke has speculated that Huang could step down "possibly staying on in another role" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/09/nvidia-power-built-around-jensen-huang-scandal-testing-formula/)). The national-security angle makes an executive-chair title hard to defend, though, and no CEO in the misconduct precedents kept one.

**Scenario 3 is the outcome that splits the two markets.** It is the board's answer to the leadership vacuum Luria described. It conflicts, however, with leaks that say "oust" rather than "transition."

**Scenario 4 is procedurally the cleanest.** A board that has just hired a former US Attorney has a reason to let him finish. If the Powell facts are confirmed, the leave most likely ends in departure, as Kalanick's did within a week.

**Scenario 5 echoes Looney in 2022**, when he survived a first review by disclosing past relationships and was brought down by a later round of allegations. Plausible concessions include an independent chair, a COO or president, a formal succession plan and board refresh, starting with Neal, who drew 13.3% opposition in June. The DOJ probe, Congress and any further allegations would keep the one-year exit risk near 30%.

**Scenario 6 requires the Powell account to collapse**, for example if the relationship predated her reporting to him or had been disclosed, and the intelligence questions to fade. Trump's support and employee loyalty help here. Even OpenAI's reinstatement of Altman came with a new board.

Several developments would raise my numbers. These include an 8-K or newsroom statement naming an interim CEO, Huang's absence from the November 17 call, Powell being placed on leave, a congressional subpoena, charges in the Megaspeed probe, and public statements from BlackRock or Vanguard. Others would lower them: a renewed board statement of confidence, Huang hosting the earnings call as CEO, investigators finding the Powell relationship disclosed or outside her reporting line, or the administration publicly clearing him.

## Conclusion

The question has moved from *whether* Huang leaves to *how*, and the "how" is what the two prediction markets actually disagree about. Huang was never protected by Nvidia's structure. He holds 3.6%, has no chair title, has no super-voting shares and can be removed by a single board vote. He was protected by the board's deference, and the governance he designed now cuts both ways. Because he refused succession planning and chose which executives met the board, there is no ready successor. That absence is now the strongest argument for keeping him and the main reason a clean ouster would be messy. Even the roughly 15% of paths in which he stays would leave Nvidia with a weakened founder, a board under pressure to refresh itself and an open DOJ probe, so "No" on these contracts does not mean a return to the status quo.

For anyone reading the markets, the Kalshi–Polymarket spread is the cleanest public price of a "founder-negotiated transition." At about 4–7% in the near term, it looks about right. Any mispricing lies in the overall levels and at the long end, where Kalshi's 96–98¢ contracts and Polymarket's 18–20¢ "No Change" successor outcome cannot both be correct, rather than between platforms. The next seven days, with a likely board decision, the November 17 earnings call and the November 18 House deadline, should resolve most of the uncertainty. The deferred-departure tail and the DOJ probe will last longer.
