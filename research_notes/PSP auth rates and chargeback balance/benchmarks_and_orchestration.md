# Independent authorization-rate benchmarks, decline drivers, network tokens, retry rules and payment orchestration (as of late October 2026)

Notes on sources: most numbers here come from vendors. Card networks, PSPs, orchestrators and fraud vendors all sell the fix for the problem they measure. Each finding says who published it and whether it is (a) a network or regulator primary source, (b) an independent survey or analyst, or (c) vendor marketing. Where a figure came only from a search-engine summary or secondary coverage and I could not open the primary document, it is marked "secondary / not verified against primary".

---

## Q1. Credible benchmark auth rates: CNP vs card-present, by region, card type, domestic vs cross-border, industry/MCC (incl. high-risk and MCC 6051), and recurring vs one-off

### Takeaway
No independent, methodology-disclosed public dataset of card authorization rates broken out by region, card type, MCC or channel exists. The Federal Reserve, EBA/ECB and the networks publish fraud rates, not approval rates. The approval-rate "benchmarks" that circulate (CNP about 85–92%, card-present about 98–99%, cross-border 5–15+ points lower than domestic) are vendor estimates with no stated methodology. The only methodologically footnoted CNP auth-rate figures come from Visa, and only as token-vs-PAN deltas. The definition of "auth rate" also differs between sources (first attempt vs last attempt vs end-to-end), and this alone moves the numbers by several points.

### Cited Findings
**Overall CNP / CP ranges (all vendor, none with a stated methodology)**
- Gr4vy (orchestration vendor, 2026 post): "General e-commerce card-not-present transactions typically see authorization rates between 85 and 92 percent". Well-optimized CNP merchants reach "90 to 95 percent". "Optimized global merchants using tokenization and smart routing can reach 91 to 96 percent or higher". "Domestic online cards in North America generally perform at 92 to 95 percent for well-configured setups". For domestic transactions generally, a "healthy benchmark … typically sits above the mid-to-high 90 percent range". The article cites no source for any of these. — [Gr4vy, Top 12 payment performance benchmarks for 2026](https://gr4vy.com/posts/top-12-payment-performance-benchmarks-for-2026/)
- Payments & Risk reference page (aggregator, 2026): typical US e-commerce CNP auth rate 85–90%, and card-present (retail/in-person) auth rates 98–99%. Secondary / not verified against primary. — [paymentsandrisk.com benchmarks](https://paymentsandrisk.com/docs/reference/benchmarks/)
- Another payments glossary puts average CNP authorization rates "typically between 85% and 95%" and warns that providers reporting only first-attempt authorization as "approval rate" overstate the gap compared with providers reporting true end-to-end approval. — [PXP glossary: approval rate](https://www.pxp.io/payments-glossary/approval-rate)

**Definitions matter (primary)**
- Visa's token footnote defines auth rate as "approved authorizations divided by total authorization attempts based upon first attempt of a unique transaction". — [Visa Acceptance, Token Management Service](https://www.visaacceptance.com/en/solutions/payment-services/token-management-service.html). A separate search summary reported that another Visa page uses the *last* attempt of a unique transaction. I could not verify which page uses which. — [Visa.com, manage tokenization](https://www.visa.com/en-us/solutions/acceptance/process-payments/manage-tokenization)
- Primer (vendor) defines authorization rate as payments reaching a success status divided by payments that reached a processor. This excludes payments blocked before the processor, for example by fraud screening. — [Primer docs, metrics](https://primer.io/docs/observability/metrics)

**Domestic vs cross-border**
- Gr4vy: "International and cross-border cards typically run 5 to 15 percentage points lower than domestic rates". No source cited. — [Gr4vy benchmarks](https://gr4vy.com/posts/top-12-payment-performance-benchmarks-for-2026/)
- Gr4vy (separate post): "Domestic acquiring commonly delivers 10 to 20% higher approval rates than cross-border processing", attributed only to unnamed "widely cited industry figures". — [Gr4vy, payment orchestration statistics 2026](https://gr4vy.com/posts/payment-orchestration-statistics-for-2026-market-size-adoption-and-merchant-results/)
- Regulator context on why issuers decline cross-border: the joint EBA–ECB report (Dec 2025) found card payment fraud was about 17 times higher when the payee was outside the EEA, where SCA is not legally required and is often not applied. Total card fraud was about €1.3bn, at a value fraud rate of about 0.033%. Total reported EEA payment fraud rose from €3.4bn (2022) and €3.5bn (2023) to €4.2bn (2024). These figures come from secondary summaries of the report. — [ECB press release, 15 Dec 2025](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2025/html/ecb.pr251215~e133d9d683.en.html); [EBA press release](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/joint-eba-ecb-report-payment-fraud-strong-authentication-remains-effective-fraudsters-are-adapting); [EBA-ECB report PDF](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/intro/publications/pdf/ecb.ebaecb202512.en.pdf)

**By card type / recurring vs one-off**
- Stripe churn data (reported period Sept 2024–Aug 2025), via third-party write-ups only. I did not find the figures on Stripe's own pages. Annual involuntary (payment-failure) churn by payment method is prepaid cards 23% (highest) vs credit cards 6% (lowest). By business model, B2C has 39% annual churn, of which 9% is involuntary (24% of total churn), and B2B has 38% annual churn, of which 6% is involuntary (16% of total churn). — [Churnkey, involuntary churn benchmarks (citing Stripe)](https://churnkey.co/blog/involuntary-churn-benchmarks); [FlyCode, churn benchmarks for SaaS](https://www.flycode.com/blog/churn-benchmarks-for-saas-businesses)
- A different vendor dataset (Redux, from its own accounts, not Stripe) reports first-payment failure rates of 66.8% for prepaid, 45.4% for debit and 21.1% for credit. This is a different metric with an unclear sample, and should not be mixed with the Stripe figures. — [Redux Payments, involuntary churn guide](https://www.reduxpayments.com/blog/involuntary-churn-guide)
- Recurly publishes a churn/decline benchmark (decline rates, top decline reasons, industries most affected, recovery rates by decline reason), but it is gated behind a form and I could not read it. — [Recurly churn benchmark report (gated)](https://info.recurly.com/publishing-churn-benchmark-report); [Recurly churn-rate benchmarks](https://www.recurly.com/research/churn-rate-benchmarks?faq=5)
- Churnkey (vendor) State of Retention 2025: involuntary (payment-failure) churn "can easily comprise 40% of your churn, if not more". — [Churnkey, State of Retention 2025](https://churnkey.co/reports/state-of-retention-2025)
- Gr4vy: "60 to 70 percent of card declines are potentially recoverable" ("research", unnamed), and "one in four retried transactions can be recovered" (no source). — [Gr4vy benchmarks](https://gr4vy.com/posts/top-12-payment-performance-benchmarks-for-2026/)

**High-risk verticals / MCC 6051 (crypto purchases)**
- I found no published approval-rate figure for MCC 6051. The available material is qualitative and mostly from crypto on-ramp vendors. Crypto purchases are coded as quasi-cash (MCC 6051). One vendor says Visa and Mastercard moved crypto purchases into this category in January 2018. Many issuers treat 6051 on credit cards as a cash advance, which brings a fee and interest from day one, and some block the category outright. Bank of America's 2018 ban on crypto purchases with credit cards (not debit) is a documented example. Credit and debit often behave differently at the same issuer. — [Banxa, why did my bank block my crypto purchase](https://banxa.com/learn/buying-crypto/why-did-my-bank-block-my-crypto-purchase); [Banxa, buying crypto with a credit card](https://banxa.com/learn/how-to-buy-and-sell/how-to-buy-crypto-with-credit-card)
- Declines in high-risk MCCs can come from the acquirer as well as the issuer. A "merchant override decline" means the issuer approved but the acquirer blocked. — [Chargeback.io, merchant override decline](https://www.chargeback.io/es/blog/what-is-merchant-override-decline)

**Merchant self-reported data (independent survey, 2026)**
- MRC / Visa Acceptance 2026 Global eCommerce Payments & Fraud Report (survey of 1,278 merchants in 37 countries, fielded Nov–Dec 2025 by independent firm B2B International). "Payment success rate" and revenue are the two most important metrics: more than half rate them "extremely important" out of 14 metrics. Authorization rate is a secondary KPI. The order-rejection-rate figures sit in an image chart (Fig. 27) and are not extractable as text. North American merchants report a significantly lower order rejection rate than other regions, and mid-market merchants report higher order rejection rates than SMBs and enterprises. — [MRC/Visa 2026 Global eCommerce Payments & Fraud Report (PDF)](https://www.visaacceptance.com/content/dam/documents/campaign/fraud-report/2026_Global_eCommerce_PaymentsandFraudReport.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a report, the defensible framing is: "industry-quoted CNP auth rates are about 85–95% (vendor estimates, no disclosed methodology); card-present is typically quoted at about 98–99%; cross-border CNP carries a mid-single-digit to mid-teens point penalty." State clearly that no independent public panel exists. The most rigorous numbers are each large PSP's internal data, and none of them publish a vertical or MCC matrix.
- The 17x cross-border fraud multiple in the EBA–ECB data is the best regulator-grade explanation for issuer conservatism on cross-border CNP. It explains why local acquiring and routing generate uplift, even though it is not itself an approval-rate statistic.
- MCC 6051 approval rates are likely structurally lower on credit cards than debit, because of issuer category blocks and cash-advance treatment. This is an inference from the qualitative evidence; I found no published rate.

### Gaps
- No independent region-by-card-type-by-MCC approval matrix was found. The Federal Reserve Payments Study, EBA/ECB and network rule summaries publish volumes and fraud rates, not approval rates.
- I found no Edgar Dunn, Juniper or Datos public figure for auth rates by segment. Their reports are paywalled.
- No credible published figures for gaming, travel, digital-goods or crypto (MCC 6051) approval rates. iGaming-specific ranges (for example LATAM 60–75%, Africa 50–70%) appeared in search summaries without an identifiable primary source, so I excluded them.
- Recurly's decline-rate-by-industry benchmark is gated. No Chargebee decline benchmark was found.
- Visa's own 2025 token metrics (a reported "4.8% auth uplift / 39.4% lower fraud rate" in tokenized CNP 2025) appeared only in a search-engine summary attributed to a Solidgate blog. I could not verify them against any Visa document, so they should not be used without confirmation.

## Q2. Decline drivers: soft vs hard share, most common reason codes, false-decline share, and false-decline cost vs actual fraud

### Takeaway
Insufficient funds (51) and generic "Do Not Honor" (05) dominate decline mixes. The most-cited single figure is an Ethoca study putting insufficient funds at about 44% of declines, but it is only available secondhand. I found no primary dataset quantifying the soft vs hard split. False declines are widely estimated to cost far more than fraud. The most recent headline figure is Datos Insights' modeled $213bn globally in 2025, rising to $297bn by 2029, available via secondary coverage. Merchant-side survey data (MRC 2026) shows 65% of merchants estimate their own false-positive rate at 2–10%, with signs it is rising.

### Cited Findings
**Reason codes (vendor-sourced)**
- Insufficient funds / over limit (code 51) is cited as about 44% of declines in an Ethoca study. One vendor gives 44.4% of issuer declines on CNP. I could not locate the original Ethoca study. — [Chargeback Gurus, decline codes](https://www.chargebackgurus.com/blog/credit-card-decline-codes); [BeastInsights, decline codes 05/51/54](https://beastinsights.com/blog/credit-card-decline-code)
- Estimates of the combined 05 + 51 share conflict: ">75% of credit card declines" (one vendor) vs "60% of all declines" (another). Neither cites a primary source. Vendors also disagree on whether 05 or 51 is #1. — [Chargeflow, decline codes](https://www.chargeflow.io/blog/credit-card-decline-codes); [BeastInsights, issuer decline](https://beastinsights.com/blog/issuer-decline)
- Vendors also disagree on how to classify 05. Some list it as soft and retryable; others group it with lost/stolen as "stop retrying". Visa's own rule taxonomy (Q5) puts 05 in neither the never-retry Category 1 list nor the Category 2 list cited by Payway. — [BeastInsights, soft vs hard](https://beastinsights.com/blog/soft-decline-vs-hard-decline); [Payway, Visa excessive reattempts](https://www.payway.com/visa-excessive-reattempts-rule-fees)
- Incorrect card information is cited (attributed to Stripe) as about 1 in 5 declines. Secondary / not verified. — [Stripe, complete list of decline codes](https://stripe.com/resources/more/a-complete-list-of-decline-codes)

**Primary network categories that define "hard" vs "soft" (see Q5 for detail)**
- Visa Category 1 "never retry" (issuer will never approve): 04, 07, 12, 14, 15, 41, 43, 46, 57, R0, R1, R3. Category 2 (retry allowed, up to 15 times in 30 days): 03, 19, 51, 59, 61, 62, 65, 75, 86, 91, 93, 96, N3, N4. — [Payway, Visa Excessive Reattempts Rule](https://www.payway.com/visa-excessive-reattempts-rule-fees)

**False declines: size and cost**
- Datos Insights estimate (cited by The Fintech Times, Aug 2026): false declines cost the industry $213bn in 2025, projected to reach $297bn by 2029. No methodology was given in the article. The figures are modeled foregone revenue, not observed losses. — [The Fintech Times / BPC analysis](https://thefintechtimes.com/bpc-banks-count-fraud-losses-but-not-what-false-declines-cost/)
- Earlier Datos vintages (secondary, different scope): average e-commerce false-decline rate of 1.51% of sales, with losses approaching about $265bn by 2027. — [ecommercegermany.com, "The $265 billion mistake"](https://ecommercegermany.com/blog/false-decline-ecommerce/). These do not reconcile with the 2025/2029 series, so treat them as separate vintages.
- Issuer-side ratio (vendor BPC, Aug 2026 interview): a typical mid-sized issuer on legacy rules has a fraud-to-false-decline ratio of about 1:30. Modern real-time platforms keep false-decline rates below 0.5%, while static rules can exceed 5%. Banks moving to adaptive decisioning "consistently report false decline reductions of 20 per cent to 40 per cent". Modeled lost interchange is about $160k/yr for a mid-sized issuer, assuming 10m debit transactions a month and a 0.5-point false-decline rate. — [The Fintech Times / BPC](https://thefintechtimes.com/bpc-banks-count-fraud-losses-but-not-what-false-declines-cost/)
- Older and loosely sourced ratios: "false declines cost about 13x more than fraud" (for example, about $443bn in false declines vs about $32bn in fraud) is widely repeated without a primary source. Treat it as a dated rule of thumb. — [Corgi Labs, false decline tax](https://www.corgilabs.ai/insights/false-decline-tax). A US-only figure of about $81bn in false declines (2023), attributed to PYMNTS Intelligence, is compared with about $48bn of global e-commerce fraud. Secondary. — [BeastInsights, $81B false decline](https://beastinsights.com/blog/false-decline)
- Nuvei (PSP) frames false declines as "a $430 billion problem". Vendor, methodology not checked. — [Nuvei](https://www.nuvei.com/posts/false-declines-a-430-billion-problem-that-demands-strategic-risk-management)

**Merchant survey (independent fieldwork, network-sponsored)**
- MRC/Visa 2026 report: about 65% of merchants estimate their false-positive ("customer insult") rate on e-commerce orders at 2–10%, mostly at the lower end. Significantly more merchants now cite 2–5% and fewer cite ≤2% than a year earlier, which "may indicate rising numbers of customer insults". 41% of MRC members report false-positive rates below 2%, while 44% of non-MRC enterprises report rates above 5%. Average fraud rate by order rose significantly, from 3.0% (2025 survey) to 3.5% (2026 survey). Methodology: 1,278 merchants, 37 countries, fielded Nov–Dec 2025 by B2B International. — [MRC/Visa 2026 Global eCommerce Payments & Fraud Report (PDF)](https://www.visaacceptance.com/content/dam/documents/campaign/fraud-report/2026_Global_eCommerce_PaymentsandFraudReport.pdf)
- The prior (2025) MRC report noted merchants had reduced false-positive rates, with a decline in the share reporting rates above 10%. — [MRC 2025 Global Fraud and Payments Report (PDF)](https://info.merchantriskcouncil.org/hubfs/Documents/Reports/Fraud%20Reports/2025_Global_Fraud_and_Payments_Report.pdf)

### Inferences
- Even the most conservative modeled false-decline cost ($213bn in 2025, Datos) is well above observed fraud losses: for example, EEA card fraud of about €1.3bn at 0.033% per EBA–ECB, and global e-commerce fraud figures in the tens of billions. The direction (false declines > fraud) is consistent across sources. The multiple (13x, 30:1 and so on) is not robust.
- The 2026 MRC data (fraud rate by order up, false-positive estimates drifting up, plus merchant fraud-tool usage falling under cost pressure) suggests a 2026 environment of both more fraud and more insults. That combination argues for better decisioning rather than simply tighter rules.
- The large "Do Not Honor" share means much of the decline mix is opaque. This is the space that network tokens, 3DS data-sharing and issuer-specific routing claim to address.

### Gaps
- No primary dataset on the soft vs hard decline split. Vendor claims exist, but none with a traceable methodology.
- I could not access the original Ethoca study, Datos Insights reports, or Riskified/Signifyd/Forter/Ravelin primary false-decline studies (paywalled or not surfaced). The Ravelin Global Payments Report 2026 is referenced by Gr4vy, but I did not read it.
- The MRC 2026 numeric order-rejection and chargeback-rate tables are in non-extractable charts.
- A search summary attributed to Datos a finding that only 74% of US and 61% of UK merchants track false declines as a KPI. I could not tie this to a specific primary or secondary URL, so it is excluded from the findings.
- Merchants self-report false-decline rates lower than their transaction data shows (a recurring vendor claim). No primary study was located.

## Q3. Network-token uplift according to Visa and Mastercard themselves: current numbers and sources

### Takeaway
Visa's footnoted, primary figure is still a 4.6% higher CNP authorization rate for tokens vs PAN (VisaNet, Oct–Dec 2022, first attempt). Visa's fraud figure has ranged from 28% (FY19–FY20 data) to "40% lower fraud rate" (CPO Jack Forestell, Sept 2025, about 5 points better approval). Mastercard's figures range from "up to 3ppt" (Nov 2024 brochure) to 3–6 points (CFO Sachin Mehra, Sept 2025). An April 2026 Mastercard white paper, co-authored with Checkout.com and available via secondary coverage, reports +10.3 points on first-attempt approvals (excluding financial declines), +12.4 points in MENA, and 49% fewer fraud-related chargebacks, using Checkout.com FY2025 data. These are all network or PSP self-reported figures, not independent.

### Cited Findings
**Visa (primary)**
- "4.6% higher authorization rates on average for CNP transactions with tokens compared with PAN". Footnote: "Source: VisaNet, Oct–Dec 2022. Visa credit and debit global card-not-present transactions for tokenized vs non-tokenized credentials. Auth rate is defined as approved authorizations divided by total authorization attempts based upon first attempt of a unique transaction." — [Visa Acceptance, Token Management Service](https://www.visaacceptance.com/en/solutions/payment-services/token-management-service.html)
- On the same page, network tokenization "can reduce fraud by an average of 28%". Footnote: "VisaNet Data, Global, YoY comparison between FY19 Q4 and FY20 Q4 share of token vs non-token PV for merchants with average 1k CNP token transactions per month – Global Risk". This is old data and the metric is not clearly defined. Footprint as of 10 July 2023: 7bn+ tokens, 500+ token requestors, 189 markets, 8.5k issuers, >95% issuer coverage by payment volume. — [Visa Acceptance, TMS](https://www.visaacceptance.com/en/solutions/payment-services/token-management-service.html)
- Visa.com (US acceptance page) cites "a 4.3% increase in authorization rates with network tokens" and 10bn tokens provisioned, plus "$650M in fraud savings" (citing Visa's June 2024 "10 Billionth Token" release). My fetch showed the footnote period as Oct–Dec 2022, but a search summary reported Oct–Dec 2023. This inconsistency is unresolved. — [Visa.com, manage tokenization](https://www.visa.com/en-us/solutions/acceptance/process-payments/manage-tokenization)
- Visa CPO Jack Forestell (Goldman Sachs Communacopia, 9 Sept 2025): tokenized transactions are about 5 percentage points more likely to complete without an approval hiccup and have a 40% lower fraud rate. About 1bn credentials were provisioned in the first five years and 14bn in the next five. About 100% of Visa face-to-face payments are tokenized (EMV/contactless). Fraud at POS with tokenized chip/contactless fell "almost 90%". Roughly half of digital transactions remain to be tokenized ("we've got 50% to go"). — [Payments Dive, 10 Sept 2025](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/visa-mastercard-push-more-tokenization/759762)
- Visa CEMEA cites a 31% fraud reduction (FY23 data), and a Visa Malta page cites "up to 50%" fraud reduction for European data. These are search-snippet level and were not fetched. — [Visa CEMEA network tokens](https://globalclient.visa.com/cemea-network-tokens); [Visa Malta, Visa Token Service](https://www.visa.com.mt/partner-with-us/payment-technology/visa-token-service.html)
- A Visa/Adyen case study puts token uplift at 2–7% over PAN, about 3% on average (secondary). — [Visa–Adyen case study (PDF)](https://www.visa.com/vcom-assets/content/dam/visa/reimagine-visa/business/programs/documents/visa.-adyen-case-study.pdf)

**Mastercard**
- Mastercard CFO Sachin Mehra (8 Sept 2025): tokenized transactions show a 3–6 percentage point increase in cardholder spending vs non-tokenized, attributed to more approvals. Other coverage paraphrases this as "3–6 ppt approval improvement". — [Payments Dive, 10 Sept 2025](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/visa-mastercard-push-more-tokenization/759762)
- Mastercard Nov 2024 brochure: network tokens can increase approval rates "by up to 3ppt". Search-snippet level. — [Mastercard, Tokenization Revolution Nov 2024 (PDF)](https://www.mastercard.com/content/dam/public/mastercardcom/gateway/other/Tokenization_Revolution_Nov_2024.pdf)
- Mastercard and Checkout.com white paper "Network tokenization: powering the e-commerce of today and tomorrow" (April 2026). Checkout.com merchants using network tokens saw a +10.3 ppt approval rate (FY2025, first attempts, excluding financial declines, Checkout.com network tokens vs cards), +12.4 ppt in MENA, a 49% reduction in fraud-related chargebacks, and +7.2% gross sales revenue. The PDF and Mastercard page returned 403 to my fetch, so these figures come from Mastercard-page snippets and press coverage. One blog renders the chargeback figure as "49 fewer fraud-related chargebacks per merchant in H1 2025" rather than 49%, which is unresolved. — [Mastercard Network Tokenization White Paper, April 2026 (PDF)](https://www.mastercard.com/content/dam/mccom/eu/news-and-trends/business-thought-leadership/digital-payments/pdfs/Network_Tokenization_White_Paper_April_2026.pdf); [Mastercard network tokenization page](https://www.mastercard.com/global/en/business/payments/consumer-payments/network-and-digital-payments/network-tokenization.html); [TechAfrica News bulletin, 19 Jul 2026](https://techafricanews.com/?p=77973)
- Mastercard 2020 Australia release: tokenisation could reduce online payment fraud on Mastercard cards by 30%, with an approval increase cited around 7.5% (search snippet). — [Mastercard newsroom, July 2020](https://newsroom.mastercard.com/news/ap/en/newsroom/press-releases/en/2020/july/mastercard-strengthens-online-payment-security-credentials)
- An average 2.1% authorization uplift for Mastercard tokens vs PAN is widely repeated (for example by Solidgate) but I could not trace it to a Mastercard primary document. — [Solidgate, network tokenization auth rates](https://solidgate.com/blog/network-tokenization-authorization-rates/)
- Checkout.com's own product page claims an "11% average increase of acceptance rates with Visa and MasterCard" (no methodology) and 200m+ network tokens across 400+ enterprise merchants. — [Checkout.com, Network Tokens](https://www.checkout.com/products/network-tokens)

**Adoption (independent survey)**
- MRC/Visa 2026: 72% of merchants use one or more forms of payment tokenization. Usage is broadly similar for gateway, network and customer tokens (40–45% each). More than half of token users cite improved authorization rates as a reason, behind security/data protection. Enterprises are much more likely to use network tokens than mid-market or SMB merchants. — [MRC/Visa 2026 report (PDF)](https://www.visaacceptance.com/content/dam/documents/campaign/fraud-report/2026_Global_eCommerce_PaymentsandFraudReport.pdf)
- Issuer coverage in practice (vendor live data, Sept 2025): about 89.9% of attempted network tokenizations were fully supported by issuers, and about 10.1% failed because the issuer does not support network tokens. — [Evervault, evaluating network tokens](https://evervault.com/blog/evaluating-network-tokens-are-they-worth-it)

### Inferences
- The best "current" Visa citation is two-part: the footnoted 4.6% (Oct–Dec 2022 CNP, first attempt) and the Sept 2025 executive statement of about 5 points / 40% lower fraud, which has no footnote. The 28–30% fraud figure the brief mentions rests on FY19–FY20 data and should be labeled as old.
- Mastercard's 10.3 ppt is from a single acquirer's merchant base (Checkout.com), measured on first attempts excluding financial declines. It is plausibly inflated relative to network-wide averages by selection effects: token adopters tend to be sophisticated enterprise merchants. Mastercard's own network-wide framing (up to 3ppt; 3–6 points) is the more conservative reference.
- Uplift claims are not additive with orchestration claims. Several orchestrator case studies bundle tokens with routing.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-network, non-PSP) measurement of network-token auth uplift was found.
- I could not open the Mastercard April 2026 white paper PDF (HTTP 403) to confirm methodology, or Visa's June 2024 "10 billionth token" release for the "$40bn incremental e-commerce" figure.
- No Visa footnoted 2025 or 2026 token uplift figure was found. Forestell's "about 5 points / 40%" comes from a conference remark.

## Q4. Orchestration platforms: what they offer and claim for auth uplift (multi-PSP routing, cascading/failover, network tokens), independent evidence, and trade-offs

### Takeaway
Vendor-claimed uplift from multi-PSP routing and failover clusters around 2–5 points in average claims, with case-study outliers of 6–11%. Fallback/cascade "recovery" claims (about 17–30% of declined or qualifying transactions recovered) use different denominators and are not comparable to auth-rate uplift. I found no independent, audited evidence (no Gartner Market Guide, Edgar Dunn or similar surfaced) quantifying orchestration uplift. PSP-native orchestration is still immature: Stripe Orchestration remains in private preview as of Oct 2026, card-only, and disputes/settlement stay with the third-party processor. Adyen pursues single-PSP AI optimization (Uplift) rather than routing to competitors. The main trade-offs are token portability (who owns the TRID), duplicated token/vault fees, PCI scope, and loss of single-PSP ML signal (the last is an inference).

### Cited Findings
**Vendor claims by platform**
- **Primer** (vendor): homepage claims a 5% average authorization-rate uplift and "30% of transactions recovered using fallbacks". The Fallbacks page claims merchants average a 20% recovery rate when immediately retrying on an alternative processor. Q1 2025 release notes say Fallbacks recovered over $35m for merchants in the prior year. — [Primer, boost payment success](https://primer.io/why-primer/boost-payment-success); [Primer Fallbacks](https://primer.io/optimize/fallbacks); [Primer, What we released Q1 2025](https://primer.io/blog/what-we-released-q1-2025)
  - Banxa (crypto on-ramp) via Fallbacks: a trade-site account says 22% success on previously declined transactions and $7.6m recovered, while Primer's site says "+17% of qualifying payments". These are inconsistent. — [Primer, fallback strategy blog](https://primer.io/blog/why-merchants-should-build-a-fallback-strategy)

**Trade-offs (sourced)**
- **Token portability / TRID**: when a PSP owns the token requestor ID, the PSP owns the network tokens, so switching PSPs means re-collecting cards. A merchant-owned TRID (via a standalone token provider or orchestrator) lets one token work across PSPs. Migrating between token providers is "enormously painful". Multi-PSP setups can tokenize the same card once per PSP and pay each time. — [Evervault](https://evervault.com/blog/evaluating-network-tokens-are-they-worth-it)
- **Fees**: example network-token fee of about $0.03 per tokenized transaction. Card Account Updater costs about $0.15–0.25 per update (more than 10x token lifecycle costs). The page says (unlinked) that Visa reports a 0.10% CNP interchange reduction for tokens, and notes penalties for non-tokenized transactions such as Visa's Secure Credential Framework fee. Expect no "material impacts for at least 3–6 months". — [Evervault](https://evervault.com/blog/evaluating-network-tokens-are-they-worth-it)
- **Liability / ops split**: with Stripe Orchestration, disputes and settlement must be handled in each third-party processor's dashboard, and processing fees and loss liability follow each processor's contract. — [Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/orchestration)
- **Build cost**: a vendor blog cites a Gartner TCO view that in-house orchestration costs $2m+ upfront, with 50–70% of total cost in maintenance. I could not verify this against Gartner. — [FlyCode, smart payment orchestration](https://www.flycode.com/blog/smart-payment-orchestration-from-simple-rules-to-ai-unlocking-failed-payment-boost-with-multi-processor-strategy)

### Inferences
- A reasonable synthesis: for a merchant already on a top-tier PSP with network tokens, incremental uplift from adding multi-PSP routing is likely low single digits. Gr4vy's +2.8 points and Ferryhopper's about +2.1 points are the most concrete case studies. Claims of 5–11% probably bundle tokens, local acquiring and retry logic, or start from a weak baseline.
- Fallback "recovery rate" (for example 20% of declines recovered) converts to auth-rate uplift as recovery rate × decline rate × eligible share. With a 10% decline rate and only soft declines eligible, a 20% recovery is about 1–2 points of overall auth rate. This is an inference, not a vendor claim.
- Cross-PSP cascading interacts with network retry rules (Q5). A retry on a second acquirer is still a reattempt on the same PAN in Visa and Mastercard's eyes, so it counts toward the 15/30 (Visa) and 10/24h–35/30d (Mastercard) thresholds. Retrying hard declines (Visa Category 1, Mastercard MAC 03/21) through a different PSP creates fee exposure.
- Splitting volume across PSPs dilutes each PSP's ML training data and issuer relationships (volume-tiered pricing, issuer-specific optimizations such as Stripe's Enhanced Issuer Network). This is an inference from how PSP-native features are described; I found no quantified source.

## Q5. Network rules on retries that constrain "smart retry" (Visa reattempt limits and fees; Mastercard MACs and Transaction Processing Excellence)

### Takeaway
Visa (since April 2022) charges for any retry of a Category 1 "never retry" decline, and for each retry after the 15th in a rolling 30 days on Category 2/3 declines. The cited fee is $0.10 domestic plus $0.05 cross-border, though other sources disagree on amounts. Mastercard's Transaction Processing Excellence (TPE) "excessive authorization attempts" fee applies above about 10 attempts in 24h or 35 in 30 days on the same PAN after the first decline (7/35 in Brazil/LATAM), at about $0.30–0.55 per excess attempt (US $0.50 from Jan 2025). Mastercard Merchant Advice Codes (MACs) say whether and when to retry (01 update credentials, 02 retry later, 03 never retry, 21 recurring cancelled, 24–30 retry after 1h to 10 days). Secondary sources report that from Jan 2026 Mastercard charges a fee on declined CNP transactions returning MAC 03 or 21 even on the first attempt. Amounts vary by region and acquirer and should be confirmed against network bulletins.

### Cited Findings
**Visa**
- "As of April 2022, Visa assess fees on decline reattempts." Excessive = (a) any reattempt of a transaction that returned a Category 1 (never retry) code, or (b) each reattempt after the 15th in a 30-day period for Category 2 or 3. "The Visa Rules allow no more than 15 reattempts in a 30-day period." — [Payway, Visa Excessive Reattempts Rule & Fees](https://www.payway.com/visa-excessive-reattempts-rule-fees)
- Category 1 (issuer will never approve; "account never existed or is permanently blocked including lost or stolen"): 04, 07, 12, 14, 15, 41, 43, 46, 57, R0, R1, R3. "Re-attempt not allowed … Any reattempt to authorize will be assessed a fee". Category 2 (temporary; up to 15 reattempts in 30 days): 03, 19, 51, 59, 61, 62, 65, 75, 86, 91, 93, 96, N3, N4. — [Payway](https://www.payway.com/visa-excessive-reattempts-rule-fees)
- Fees per Payway: "$0.10 charge per re-attempts over 15" ("VS RAF Excessive Reattempts Domestic"), plus "an additional $0.05 per transaction" cross-border. Other secondary sources quote different amounts (for example about $0.25, or "$25"), likely reflecting acquirer markups or misreporting. — [Payway](https://www.payway.com/visa-excessive-reattempts-rule-fees); [CancelFlow, decline code retry limits](https://cancelflow.dev/blog/decline-code-retry-limits)

**Mastercard**
- TPE Excessive Authorizations fee: a monthly charge for too many authorization attempts on the same PAN in a short period (retries after declines, card testing, system errors). Typical thresholds are "10 retries in 24 hours and 35 retries in 30 days", varying by market (Brazil/LATAM 7/35). The fee is $0.30–$0.55 (or local equivalent) per excess attempt, with the US rate rising to $0.50 in January 2025. It is billed about two months after the activity. — [IXOPAY, Mastercard TPE excessive authorizations fee](https://www.ixopay.com/payment-knowledge-hub/mastercard-transaction-processing-excellence-excessive-authorizations-fee)
- Braintree's 2024 network-update notice gives a EUR-denominated fee of €0.55 per transaction once the 35-attempt threshold is exceeded (search snippet). Another processor lists $0.15 per attempt for 10-in-24h breaches. Amounts vary by region and date. — [Braintree network updates 2024 (MC)](https://developer.paypal.com/braintree/articles/risk-and-security/compliance/network-updates/2024/mc-su24); [TabaPay, network fees](https://developers.tabapay.com/docs/network-authorization-best-practices)
- Secondary report of a 2026 change: "starting in January 2026, Mastercard will begin charging this fee to all declined card-not-present transactions with MAC decline codes 03 or 21, not just on re-try attempts". Moneris' Jan 2026 fee table lists a separate "Mastercard – Merchant Advice Code" fee (about $0.113 in one column and $0.2825 in another; column meaning unclear). I could not open either page directly (403 / snippet only). — [Merchant Cost Consulting, Mastercard TPE fees](https://merchantcostconsulting.com/lower-credit-card-processing-fees/mastercard-transaction-processing-excellence-fees/); [Moneris, payment card network fee updates](https://www.moneris.com/en/pcno-fee-updates/)
- MAC meanings (processor docs): 01 = new account information available. 02 = cannot approve at this time, try again later. 03 = do not try again. 21 = recurring/payment agreement cancelled by cardholder, do not retry. 24 = retry after 1 hour; 25 = after 24 hours; 26 = after 2 days; 27 = after 4 days; 28 = after 6 days; 29 = after 8 days; 30 = after 10 days. — [Braintree, Merchant Advice Codes](https://developer.paypal.com/braintree/docs/reference/general/merchant-responses/merchant-advice-codes/); [Peach Payments, MAC](https://support.peachpayments.com/support/solutions/articles/47001283346-mac-merchant-advice-codes); [Payabl, Mastercard MACs](https://docs.payabl.com/docs/mastercard-merchant-advice-codes); [TabaPay, MAC](https://developers.tabapay.com/docs/merchant-advice-code-mac)
- PayPal guidance refers to an Excessive Reattempt fee "after 20 retries in 30 days". This conflicts with the 10/35 thresholds and probably reflects an older or different rule. PayPal also warns that retrying "do not retry" declines incurs fees. — [PayPal, avoid excessive retries penalties](https://paypal.com/us/brc/article/avoid-excessive-retries-penalties)

### Inferences
- Combined, the rules cap any "smart retry" or cascading design. Per PAN: never retry Visa Category 1 or Mastercard MAC 03/21. Honor MAC 24–30 timing. Keep Visa retries at 15 or fewer per 30 days and Mastercard at 10 or fewer per 24h and 35 or fewer per 30 days. Count retries across all acquirers and orchestrator fallbacks, because the networks count by PAN and merchant, not by PSP.
- The reported Jan 2026 Mastercard change to charge on first-attempt MAC 03/21 CNP declines (if confirmed) shifts the cost toward merchants with stale card-on-file data. That strengthens the case for network tokens and account updater ahead of any retry logic.
- Per-attempt fees are small (cents) relative to recovered revenue, but they compound for card-testing attacks and naive dunning. The larger hidden cost is reputational: issuer risk scores and acquirer monitoring.

### Gaps
- I could not read Visa's Core Rules or Mastercard's TPE program guide directly. Rule details come from acquirer and processor pages. Visa Category 3 and 4 code lists and their exact treatment were not confirmed from a primary source.
- I could not confirm the exact amount and scope of the Jan 2026 Mastercard MAC 03/21 fee from a Mastercard document. The thresholds and fees for 2026 should be checked against current acquirer bulletins.
- No source quantified how much auth-rate uplift retry strategies forgo because of these caps.
