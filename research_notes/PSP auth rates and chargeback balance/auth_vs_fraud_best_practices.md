# Balancing Card Authorization Rate Against Fraud and Chargeback Rate: Industry Best Practices (notes as of late Oct 2026)

Source-quality legend used below: [PRIMARY] = network, regulator, or the PSP's own published data/docs; [PSP] = PSP documentation or blog (has a commercial interest but often reports platform data); [VENDOR] = fraud/chargeback vendor marketing or blog (strong bias, often no methodology); [SECONDARY] = trade press or aggregator. Dates are given for every figure; older figures are labelled with their year.

## 1. Metrics: how the trade-off is defined, measured, and optimized

### Takeaway
Practitioners treat auth-vs-fraud as a cost-minimization problem across fraud losses, chargeback fees, lost margin on falsely declined good orders, review cost, and (increasingly) the shadow cost of breaching Visa VAMP / Mastercard EFM ratios. The operating point is set by tuning a risk-score threshold against margin. Stripe's published break-even-precision example and Airbnb's loss-function thresholding are the clearest public examples. The definitions of "auth rate" differ between teams, so a written metric dictionary comes first.

### Cited Findings
**Core definitions (precision / recall / false positive rate)**
- Stripe defines, for a policy such as "block if P(fraud) > 0.7": **precision** = the share of blocked transactions that are actually fraud; **recall** = the share of all fraud the policy catches; **false positive rate (FPR)** = the share of all legitimate payments that are wrongly blocked. Worked example: 4 of 6 blocked are fraud, so precision ≈ 0.66; 4 of 5 frauds caught, so recall = 0.8; 2 of 5 legit blocked, so FPR = 0.4. [PSP] — [Stripe, "A primer on machine learning for fraud detection"](https://stripe.com/en-gb-us/radar/guide)
- "Precision and recall are inversely related. When you block more transactions, the false positive rate increases while the likelihood of allowing fraud decreases." [PSP] — [Stripe Issuing docs, risk score](https://docs.stripe.com/issuing/controls/advanced-fraud-tools/stripes-risk-score)
- Stripe says margin drives tolerance. Low-margin businesses may accept more false positives. For high-margin businesses, "the lost revenue from one legitimate blocked customer may outweigh the cost of increased fraud." Businesses "need to decide how to balance the two based on their margins, growth profile, and other factors." [PSP] — [Stripe primer](https://stripe.com/en-gb-us/radar/guide)

**Deriving the operating point: expected-value / break-even thresholding**
- Stripe's break-even precision example (labelled "APPROXIMATE"): average sale $26 at 8% margin gives $2.08 profit. Loss per fraudulent sale = $23.92 product cost + $15 chargeback fee = $38.92. One fraud therefore wipes out the profit of ~18.71 good sales. Break-even precision = 1/(1+18.71) ≈ **5.07%**. Blocking is worth it only if more than ~5% of the blocked set is fraud. [PSP] — [Stripe primer](https://stripe.com/en-gb-us/radar/guide)
- Stripe Radar for Fraud Teams exposes a 0–100 risk score per payment. Merchants "tweak the threshold at which to block payments to maximize revenue" according to their fraud appetite. Radar's default thresholds "trade off optimizing for merchants' margins and keeping block rates stable across our user base." [PSP] — [Stripe primer](https://stripe.com/en-gb-us/radar/guide); [Stripe risk evaluation docs](https://stripe.com/nl-be/docs/radar/risk-evaluation)
- Airbnb (2018) applied ML-triggered *friction* (not only blocks) to suspected fraudsters, with chargebacks as the main target. It set model thresholds by **minimizing a loss function across false positives, false negatives and true positives**. [SECONDARY, older: 2018] — [InfoQ on Airbnb financial-fraud ML](https://www.infoq.com/news/2018/03/financial-fraud-ml-airbnb/)
- Manual review moves the operating point: "By sending some classes of transactions to manual review instead of blocking them outright, you can gain precision without a hit to recall." The cost is "additional human work." Targeted custom rules "typically increase recall with little cost to precision." [PSP] — [Stripe primer](https://stripe.com/en-gb-us/radar/guide)

**Guardrail metrics and measurement**
- Stripe checks new fraud models before release against **false positive rate, block rate and authorization rate**, overall and per merchant. A model with "higher recall but [that] cause[s] a spike in block rate" would be disruptive. Stripe monitors FPR and recall to keep them "within desirable ranges." [PSP] — [Stripe primer](https://stripe.com/en-gb-us/radar/guide); [Stripe risk evaluation](https://stripe.com/nl-be/docs/radar/risk-evaluation)
- The counterfactual problem: "Payments that have scores above the threshold... are blocked, and so we can't know what their outcomes would have been." Stripe says it uses statistical methods to estimate outcomes of blocked payments. [PSP] — [Stripe primer](https://stripe.com/en-gb-us/radar/guide)
- Visa's VAMP ratio is a **count-based** metric: (TC40 fraud reports + TC15 disputes) ÷ settled transactions. It is not dollar-based. Mastercard EFM is **amount-based**: fraud chargebacks ≥ USD/EUR 50,000 and fraud chargeback-to-sales ≥ 0.5%. Fraud must therefore be tracked both in bps of count and bps of value. [PSP] — [Checkout.com scheme monitoring programs](https://www.checkout.com/docs/business-operations/manage-disputes/scheme-monitoring-programs.md)
- Definitions drift: vendor KPI guides warn that payments teams often measure auth rate as eventual success including retries and re-routing, while finance uses first-attempt approvals. They recommend an "actionable" auth rate that excludes declines the merchant cannot influence, such as insufficient funds. [VENDOR] — [Primer, payment metrics & KPIs](https://primer.io/blog/payment-metrics-and-kpis); [Stripe, payment KPIs](https://stripe.com/resources/more/payment-kpis-metrics-that-reveal-where-your-revenue-is-leaking)
- Network-benchmark chargeback context: Sift's Q4 2025 Digital Trust Index puts average chargeback rates at **0.26% in Q3 2025, up 53% vs Q1 2025**. [VENDOR] — [Sift Q4 2025 index](https://sift.com/index-reports-disputes-q4-2025/)
- Consumer cost of false declines: Stripe cites a survey in which **33%** of consumers said they would not shop again at a business after a false decline. [PSP citing survey] — [Stripe primer](https://stripe.com/en-gb-us/radar/guide). ClearSale cites a 2023-edition MRC figure that **41%** of consumers who experience a false decline say they will never shop with that merchant again (2023, older; not verified against the MRC original). [VENDOR/SECONDARY] — [ClearSale/Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240306666423/en/ClearSale-EVP-to-Host-Merchant-Risk-Council-Webinar-on-Fraud-Ecosystem)
- EEA macro baseline: card and payment fraud stayed at about **0.002% of transaction value**, while total losses rose to **€4.2bn in 2024** from €3.5bn in 2023. [PRIMARY via secondary] — [EBA-ECB 2025 report PDF](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/intro/publications/pdf/ecb.ebaecb202512.en.pdf); [Plenitude summary](https://www.plenitudeconsulting.com/news-insights/eba-and-ecb-release-report-on-payment-fraud)

**Recommended metric stack** (Section 1 inference, built from the sources above)
- Gross auth rate (approved ÷ submitted). Net or first-attempt auth rate, deduplicated by order. Issuer decline rate split by reason code (05/do-not-honor, 59/suspected fraud, 51/NSF, 14/invalid). Merchant risk-engine block rate. 3DS frictionless/challenge/abandon rates. Manual review rate and review approval rate. FPR estimated from holdouts. Fraud bps by count (VAMP-relevant) and by value (EFM-, TRA- and P&L-relevant). Dispute rate by reason family (fraud vs. non-fraud/first-party). Dispute win rate. Total cost of fraud = fraud losses + chargeback fees + review cost + vendor fees + lost margin on false declines.

### Inferences
- The "optimal" point is a constrained optimization, not just an unconstrained EV threshold. Maximize expected margin (approve if P(good)×margin > P(fraud)×(cost of goods + chargeback fee + ops cost)) subject to keeping VAMP and EFM ratios below a buffer, and TRA fraud rates below exemption bands for EU flows. Near a network threshold, the effective cost of a fraud event jumps: $8 per TC40/TC15 under VAMP Excessive, possible loss of liability shift, and possible termination. That pushes the threshold toward higher recall.
- Because VAMP is count-based and EFM is value-based, a merchant with many low-ticket frauds (digital goods, gaming) is more exposed to VAMP. One with few high-ticket frauds (electronics, travel) is more exposed to EFM. Thresholds should be segmented by ticket size.
- Stripe's break-even example shows that low-margin merchants have very low break-even precision (~5%). Pure EV logic would then block aggressively. LTV-aware models (a declined good customer loses future margin, cf. the 33%/41% "won't return" figures) argue for adding customer lifetime value to the false-positive cost.

### Gaps
- No public, neutral benchmark of "false positive rate" by vertical was found. FPR is rarely published because it needs counterfactual data.
- The MRC 2026 report PDF (>10 MB) could not be parsed, so its order-rejection, manual-review and chargeback-rate benchmarks are not captured here. The same applies to the 2025 report.
- No published "cost per approved dollar" benchmark was found.

## 2. 3DS2 / SCA strategy, exemptions, liability shift, and role in network programs

### Takeaway
3DS is a scalpel, not a default. In the EU/UK, SCA is mandatory and authenticated transactions authorize at least as well. In the US, Stripe's data shows frictionless 3DS can *lower* authorization, and challenges cost conversion. Best practice is to request exemptions intelligently, send rich data to raise frictionless rates, use data-only flows where liability shift is not needed, and reserve challenges for genuinely risky traffic. Liability shift moves who pays for fraud chargebacks, but it does **not** remove TC40 fraud reports from the Visa VAMP ratio. Under Mastercard EFM, low 3DS coverage is itself a placement criterion. PSD3/PSR is politically agreed but, as of the latest sources found, not yet applicable (application expected ~2027–2028).

### Cited Findings
**Authentication performance data**
- Ravelin Global Payments Report 2026 (published 19 Mar 2026; 2025 vs 2024 data; 37 countries; survey of 1,466 enterprise merchants): 3DS success rate was slightly up overall, driven mainly by the US. Challenge success was stable. **Frictionless rates declined globally, in the US and in Europe; 28 of 37 countries (~76%) saw declining or flat frictionless rates.** **78% of merchants now use exemptions.** 3DS success by country: UK 95%, Italy 93%, Netherlands 92%, France 91%, US 88%, Germany 87%, India 72%, South Africa 67%, Brazil 36%. [VENDOR, platform data] — [Ravelin, 3D Secure rates 2026](https://www.ravelin.com/blog/3d-secure-rates-2026.md); [Ravelin Global Payments Report 2026 PDF](https://pages.ravelin.com/hubfs/PR26%20Global%20Payments%20Report%202026/Global%20Payments%20Report%202026%20by%20Ravelin.pdf)
- Ravelin authentication map (Jan–Jul 2025): global 3DS success **82%**, challenge success **76%**, frictionless rate **58%**. Europe frictionless 62%, North America 54%. Ravelin defines frictionless rate as the share of all authentication attempts authenticated without a challenge. [VENDOR] — [Ravelin 3DS global authentication map](https://www.ravelin.com/3ds-global-authentication-map-sca)
- Ravelin's November 2025 press release warned that falling frictionless approvals put peak-season sales at risk. [VENDOR] — [Ravelin press release](https://www.ravelin.com/blog/ravelin-warns-peak-season-sales-at-risk-as-frictionless-payment-approvals-decline). An earlier Ravelin piece is titled "One-fifth of payments sent to 3D Secure are lost" (date not captured). [VENDOR] — [Ravelin blog](https://www.ravelin.com/blog/one-fifth-of-payments-sent-to-3d-secure-are-lost)
- Stripe US analysis (5 Aug 2024): a subset of US businesses averaged **87%** auth before adding 3DS. Challenged-and-authenticated transactions stayed at **87%**, but **frictionless-path transactions fell to 82%**. In the US, authorization and authentication rates "tended to vary inversely," while in the EU/UK they rose together. One top-10 US issuer sent 100% of transactions frictionless. Caveats: small group of businesses, two-week test. Stripe advises against assuming EU authentication strategies transfer to other markets. [PSP] — [Stripe, Surprising findings from our analysis of 3DS transactions in the US](https://stripe.com/blog/surprising-findings-from-our-analysis-of-3ds-transactions-in-the-us)
- Forter, using Amazon and Microsoft data (2020, older; Forter sells a non-3DS approach, so it is biased): UK 3DS abandonment was 3.7% (Amazon) and 15% (Microsoft), and **over 40% in France and Spain**. "Applying frictionless 3DS reduces authorisation rates by approximately 10%." [VENDOR, 2020] — [Forter, The real cost of 3DS](https://www.forter.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Forter_Real_Cost_3DS_v2.pdf)

**Fraud effect of SCA/3DS (EEA)**
- The joint EBA-ECB payment fraud report (15 Dec 2025; data H1 2022–H2 2024) finds SCA-authenticated transactions less likely to be fraudulent, with the clearest effect for cards. Fraudsters are adapting by targeting exempted transactions and social engineering. Card fraud was ~**17x higher when the counterpart was outside the EEA**, where SCA is often not applied. About **83% of 2024 fraud value was remote**. EU/EEA card losses reached **€1.329bn in 2024, +29% YoY**. [PRIMARY via secondary] — [EBA press release](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/joint-eba-ecb-report-payment-fraud-strong-authentication-remains-effective-fraudsters-are-adapting); [EBA-ECB report PDF](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/intro/publications/pdf/ecb.ebaecb202512.en.pdf); [Electronic Payments International](https://www.electronicpaymentsinternational.com/?p=81968); [PwC Legal summary](https://legal.pwc.de/en/news/articles/reviewing-eu-payments-crypto-assets-and-ai-linked-fraud-trends-key-insights-and-expectations-from-the-esas-and-ecb); [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2025/12/22/payment-fraud-hits-e4-2bn-as-criminals-adapt-to-sca/)
- Stripe cites an EU estimate that 3DS prevents about **€900m of fraud per year** in Europe. [PSP citing EU estimate] — [Stripe US 3DS blog](https://stripe.com/blog/surprising-findings-from-our-analysis-of-3ds-transactions-in-the-us)

**PSD2 exemption mechanics (current law)**
- TRA exemption reference fraud rates (RTS on SCA, Delegated Reg. 2018/389, Annex). Remote card payments: up to **€100 requires PSP fraud rate ≤ 0.13%**, up to **€250 requires ≤ 0.06%**, up to **€500 requires ≤ 0.01%**. Credit transfers: 0.015% / 0.01% / 0.005%. Per EBA Q&A, the fraud rate is calculated per transaction type, not per band. A PSP with a 0.10% card fraud rate can therefore use TRA only up to €100. [PRIMARY] — [EBA RTS Annex table](https://eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/documents/10180/1894900/2b6be09b-f3db-40e4-b0ee-906bf65e7c82/RTS%20on%20SCA%20and%20CSC%20-%20Annex%20Table%2028%20-%20June%202017.pdf); [EBA Q&A 2018_4043](https://www.eba.europa.eu/single-rule-book-qa/qna/view/publicId/2018_4043)
- A practitioner note says MOTO, one-leg-out and merchant-initiated transaction fraud is not included in the TRA fraud-rate calculation reported to the EBA. [SECONDARY] — [Fraud Practice](https://www.fraudpractice.com/post/processing-through-acquirers-with-lower-fraud-rates-reduces-the-need-for-sca-under-psd2)
- Practical implication: merchants can lower their need for SCA by routing to acquirers with lower fraud rates. The TRA exemption belongs to the acquirer's or issuer's fraud rate, not the merchant's. [SECONDARY] — [Fraud Practice](https://www.fraudpractice.com/post/processing-through-acquirers-with-lower-fraud-rates-reduces-the-need-for-sca-under-psd2)
- Other PSD2 exemptions and out-of-scope flows: low-value (≤€30, cumulative caps), recurring same-amount, trusted beneficiaries/whitelisting, secure corporate, MIT (out of scope) and one-leg-out (best-effort). [PRIMARY; note: EUR-Lex page did not render in this session] — [Delegated Regulation (EU) 2018/389, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32018R0389)
- Ravelin recommends optimizing exemption requests rather than always asking for the highest available exemption, because issuer risk tolerances vary. It also recommends sending richer data, preparing for EMV 3DS 2.3 data fields, and using adaptive optimization that learns from issuer responses. [VENDOR] — [Ravelin 2026](https://www.ravelin.com/blog/3d-secure-rates-2026.md)

**Liability shift is conditional, and 3DS's role in network programs**
- Visa US 3DS fraud program (Checkout.com description). It covers US domestic ECI 5/6 fraud. Standard thresholds: **USD 75,000 reported fraud and a 0.9% fraud-to-sales amount ratio**. Early Warning: $50k and 0.65%. Enforcement: no fines, but **Dispute Condition 10.5 liability** (the merchant loses 3DS fraud protection) and possible disqualification. The merchant must reclassify ECI 5/6 transactions to ECI 7 within 30 days. Exit: below Standard for one month. [PSP] — [Checkout.com scheme monitoring programs](https://www.checkout.com/docs/business-operations/manage-disputes/scheme-monitoring-programs.md)
- Mastercard **EFM** placement (all criteria, previous month): ≥1,000 Mastercard sales; ≥USD/EUR 50,000 in fraud chargebacks (reason code 4837); fraud chargeback-to-sales ≥0.5%; **AND 3DS/DSRP share of clearing volume below 10% (non-regulated countries) or below 50% (regulated countries)**. 3DS is identified via PDS 0052 values 211/212/214/216/217 and DSRP via 242/246. Fines start in month 2: $500, then $1k, then $5k (months 4–6), $25k (7–11), $50k (12–18), $100k (19+). Exit after three compliant months. [PSP] — [Checkout.com](https://www.checkout.com/docs/business-operations/manage-disputes/scheme-monitoring-programs.md). **Conflict:** Signifyd describes the 3DS percentages the other way round (10% regulated / 50% non-regulated). [VENDOR] — [Signifyd](https://www.signifyd.com/?p=23048). A Chargebacks911 page says data-only transactions also count toward EFM 3DS compliance. [VENDOR] — [Chargebacks911 EFM 3DS requirements](https://chargebacks911.com/mastercard-efm-program-3-d-secure-requirements/)
- **VAMP and 3DS:** multiple vendor sources state that a 3DS-authenticated transaction that later draws a TC40 fraud report **still counts in the VAMP ratio**. Liability shift changes who pays the chargeback, not whether the fraud report counts. Since authenticated fraud generally will not produce a fraud chargeback, merchants depend on PSP-provided TC40 data to see it. Only Compelling Evidence 3.0 can exclude TC40s. [VENDOR; unverified against Visa rulebook] — [Justt, Understanding VAMP](https://justt.ai/vamp/); [Chargeback Gurus, How to reduce your VAMP ratio](https://www.chargebackgurus.com/blog/how-to-reduce-your-vamp-ratio); [Evervault](https://evervault.com/blog/mastering-3d-secure-minimizing-chargebacks-and-fraud-rates-for-payment-gateways-psps-and-payfacs)

- Provisional political agreement was reached on 27 Nov 2025. Final compromise texts went to COREPER on 23 Apr 2026 (PSD3 ST-8222-2026-INIT; PSR ST-8221-2026-INIT), followed by legal-linguistic review. ### Inferences
- **EU/UK:** SCA is mandatory, so the game is exemption optimization. Use TRA where your acquirer's fraud rate qualifies (route to low-fraud acquirers), plus low-value and MIT/recurring flagging. Apply 3DS on residual traffic with full data so issuers can go frictionless. A falling frictionless rate (Ravelin) means data quality and exemption hit rate are now the main levers.
- **US/non-SCA markets:** default to no 3DS. Invoke 3DS selectively when (a) the risk score falls in a "grey band" where liability shift is worth more than expected conversion loss, (b) the order is high-ticket and fraud-prone, or (c) the merchant must raise 3DS coverage to stay out of Mastercard EFM, or is near VAMP and wants to suppress 10.4 disputes. Stripe's 87%→82% frictionless finding means US 3DS should be A/B tested by issuer.
- Liability shift is not free insurance. Visa's US 3DS program can strip it at ≥0.9% fraud-to-sales, and authenticated fraud still counts in VAMP via TC40. Merchants must not use 3DS as a substitute for fraud screening.
- PSD3/PSR will not change day-to-day exemption strategy before ~2027–2028. Stricter TRA evidence requirements will raise the value of PSPs with demonstrably low fraud rates.

## 3. Risk-based friction, step-up, and card-testing (enumeration) defenses

### Takeaway
Leading practice is a tiered decision: approve, approve with step-up (3DS, OTP, penny-drop, ID), manual review, alternative payment method, or block. Each band is set by model score and expected value. Card-testing defenses (rate limits, CAPTCHA triggers, velocity on IP/device/BIN, monitoring declines rather than chargebacks) are now compliance-relevant because Visa VAMP has an explicit enumeration ratio.

### Cited Findings
- Airbnb (2018) used ML-triggered friction targeted at suspected fraudsters rather than blanket blocks, with thresholds set by loss-function minimization. A current Airbnb Payments Transaction Risk role describes detection combining "Machine Learning models and rule-based systems." [SECONDARY] — [InfoQ on Airbnb](https://www.infoq.com/news/2018/03/financial-fraud-ml-airbnb/); [Airbnb job posting](https://workingnomads.com/jobs/staff-software-engineer-payments-transaction-risk-airbnb)
- Uber uses **TC40 early fraud signals**, which arrive before chargebacks, to spot fraud patterns early and to train models. It uses low-friction verification challenges such as a **penny-drop** check to confirm payment-profile ownership. [PRIMARY merchant engineering blog] — [Uber Engineering, How Uber processes early chargeback signals](https://www.uber.com/blog/how-uber-processes-early-chargeback-signals)
- A former Uber fraud practitioner says early reliance on rules, human review and manual chargeback work was costly. "Verification challenges" are an increasingly popular way to reduce false positives and friction. [VENDOR blog] — [Dodgeball, Fighting fraud like the pros – Uber edition](https://blog.dodgeballhq.com/the-dodgeball-blog/fighting-fraud-like-the-pros-uber-edition)
- Rules plus ML: Stripe notes targeted rules raise recall with little precision cost. Monthly model retraining can raise recall "by as much as half a percentage point each month," and model updates improved performance "by over 20% year over year." [PSP] — [Stripe primer](https://stripe.com/en-gb-us/radar/guide)
- **Visa VAMP enumeration ratio:** enumerated authorized transactions ÷ total authorized transactions. Threshold **2,000 bps (20%)**, with a minimum of **300,000 enumerated payments/month**, globally from both the June 2025 and April 2026 dates. Enumeration data is available from the PSP. [PSP] — [Checkout.com](https://www.checkout.com/docs/business-operations/manage-disputes/scheme-monitoring-programs.md). Chargebacks911 describes the denominator as total sales, a definitional conflict, and says enumeration criteria are based on "VAAI Score confirmations" (Visa Advanced Authorization). [VENDOR] — [Chargebacks911 VAMP](https://chargebacks911.com/visa-acquirer-monitoring-program/)
- Visa's guidance tells acquirers, issuers and processors to maintain controls that quickly detect and block enumeration/account-testing attacks. [PRIMARY] — [Visa guidance to guard against enumeration](https://usa.review.visa.com/content/dam/VCOM/global/support-legal/documents/visa-guidance-to-guard-against-enumeration-sept.pdf)
- Stripe says its card-testing defenses use rate limiters, ML models and CAPTCHA triggers, adjusted when an attack is detected. [PSP] — [Stripe docs, card testing](https://docs.stripe.com/card-testing)
- J.P. Morgan advises merchants **not to rely on chargebacks to detect card testing**, because chargeback windows are long. Monitor authorization-decline patterns instead. [PRIMARY acquirer] — [J.P. Morgan card testing white paper](https://JPMorgan.com/content/dam/jpm/securities/documents/card-testing-white-paper-ada-final.pdf)
- Vendor guidance: limit attempts per session and per IP/device/velocity window, and use CAPTCHA, which some treat as temporary because it dents conversion. [VENDOR] — [Zift card testing mitigation](https://learn.zift.io/card-testing-mitigation)
- Mastercard's Scam Merchant Monitoring Program flags any merchant whose **approval rate drops ≥50 percentage points or below 30% over 72 hours** (with >25 purchases) for investigation within 72 hours. Confirmed scam merchants lose processing and are placed on MATCH. [PSP] — [Checkout.com](https://www.checkout.com/docs/business-operations/manage-disputes/scheme-monitoring-programs.md)

### Inferences
- Step-up should be priced like any other decision: the expected cost of friction (abandonment × margin) vs. the expected fraud loss avoided plus liability shift. For grey-band scores, a low-friction challenge (3DS, OTP, penny-drop, wallet redirect) usually dominates outright block.
- Card-testing defense now has a hard compliance target: keep enumerated auths well under 20% of total auths. Because the volume floor is 300k/month, small merchants are shielded from the ratio, but large digital merchants and PSP aggregators are not. Monitor decline-code spikes (e.g., 14/invalid card, CVV mismatch, AVS fail) and BIN-range velocity in near-real time.
- Approval-rate collapse (e.g., an enumeration attack flooding declines) can itself trigger Mastercard SMMP scrutiny. Real-time approval-rate alerting serves both fraud and compliance.

### Gaps
- No primary Visa document detailing how "VAAI Score confirmations" classify enumeration was retrieved.
- No quantified public data on device fingerprinting or behavioral biometrics uplift (approval or fraud) from a neutral source was found.
- No public Mastercard enumeration program details were found.

## 4. Fraud tooling choices: PSP-native vs. third-party, chargeback guarantees, valid testing

### Takeaway
PSP-native tools (Stripe Radar, Adyen RevenueProtect, PayPal FPA) benefit from network-scale data and zero integration. Third-party vendors (Forter, Riskified, Signifyd, Sift, Kount, Ravelin, SEON, Feedzai) offer cross-PSP coverage and, for some, chargeback guarantees. Almost all uplift claims are vendor-produced, uncontrolled, or commissioned. Merchants should require randomized holdouts or champion/challenger tests with matured chargeback windows before believing any "+X pp approvals, −Y% fraud" claim.

### Cited Findings
- Stripe Radar 2.0 (2018, older): early testing of upgraded models "helped reduce fraud by over 25% compared to previous models, without increasing the false positive rate." [PSP, 2018] — [Stripe Radar 2.0 blog](https://stripe.com/en-in/blog/radar-2018)
- PayPal Fraud Protection Advanced (published 9 Mar 2026). It is a pre/post analysis of only **36 merchants** onboarded "on or after 2024," three months before vs. after, with **no control group**. **58%** reduced issuer declines within three months. Of these, **76%** also improved auth rate (no magnitude given). The rest saw flat or lower auth rates from fraud-rule declines, with chargebacks stable or lower at six months. [PSP] — [PayPal, How FPA improved authorization rates](https://www.paypal.com/us/brc/article/auth-rate-increases-fraud-protection-advanced)
- Chargeback-guarantee models: Signifyd, Riskified and Forter cover fraud chargebacks on orders they approve. One blog says Forter contractually guarantees approval *and* chargeback rates. Signifyd's comparison page claims Forter has "silently retreated from the liability shift model" (competitor claim). [VENDOR] — [Ringly, Riskified alternatives](https://www.ringly.io/blog/riskified-alternatives); [Signifyd vs Forter](https://www.signifyd.com/fraud-protection-software-comparison-forter)
- An unsourced claim says Riskified raises approvals by "4–8 percentage points compared to in-house solutions." A cited Forrester TEI study claims 594% three-year ROI, but TEI studies are typically vendor-commissioned. [VENDOR; unverified] — [Ringly](https://www.ringly.io/blog/riskified-alternatives)
- Pricing for guarantee vendors varies by products, vertical, volume and average ticket. [VENDOR] — [Wiser Review, Signifyd alternatives](https://wiserreview.com/blog/signifyd-alternatives/)
- PSPs warn that a fraud tool can lower chargebacks while silently increasing false declines. This is why auth rate must be a guardrail metric in tool evaluation. [VENDOR] — [Ixopay, auth rate KPI](https://www.ixopay.com/payment-knowledge-hub/successful-authorization-rate-kpi)
- Valid evaluation requires counterfactuals for blocked traffic (Stripe uses statistical estimation of blocked-payment outcomes) and guardrails on block rate, FPR and auth rate per merchant. [PSP] — [Stripe primer](https://stripe.com/en-gb-us/radar/guide)
- Good-practice example of a controlled test: the Visa/Adyen IDX pilot compared enhanced-data transactions against a **control group** on **first-attempt** approval rate, excluding wallets, standard 3DS, MIT and AFTs (see Section 7). [PRIMARY] — [Visa–Adyen case study](https://www.visa.com/vcom-assets/content/dam/visa/reimagine-visa/business/programs/documents/visa.-adyen-case-study.pdf)

### Inferences
Requirements for valid champion/challenger or A/B tests, derived from the above:
- **Randomize at the customer/card level**, not the transaction level, to avoid retry contamination.
- **Hold out a small random slice of would-be-blocked traffic** (or use propensity/counterfactual estimation) to measure FPR.
- **Wait for dispute maturation** (fraud chargebacks and TC40s lag weeks to months) before declaring a winner. Use TC40s as earlier signals (cf. Uber).
- Measure **first-attempt** and **issuer-level** approval rates.
- Stratify by issuer, BIN country and ticket size.
- Compute **net margin** (approved good revenue − fraud − fees − review − vendor cost), not just one KPI.
- Note that a vendor guarantee changes the incentive: the vendor declines more when it bears the loss. Compare approval rates on matched traffic.
- PSP-native tools lose visibility when a merchant multi-acquires. Third-party/orchestration-layer tools see all traffic but lack issuer-response data the PSP sees. Many large merchants run both, using the PSP score as a feature.

### Gaps
- No independent, peer-reviewed or third-party-audited head-to-head comparison of Forter, Riskified, Signifyd, Sift, Kount, Ravelin, SEON, Feedzai, Adyen RevenueProtect or Stripe Radar was found.
- No Adyen RevenueProtect quantitative results were retrieved in this session.

## 5. Issuer-side levers that raise approvals while lowering fraud

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced "raise auth AND keep fraud flat or lower" levers send issuers more and better data: EMV 3DS priority data elements, the 3DS Method URL, Visa IDX/Data Only (now DCAP) and network tokens. Next come correct stored-credential/MIT flags and disciplined retry behaviour that honours merchant advice codes. Visa's own data shows multi-point approval lifts from better data, and a 2025–26 Visa/Adyen pilot shows 170–310 bps lifts with stable fraud.

### Cited Findings
**Richer data via 3DS / Data Only**
- Visa "Better Data" guide (© 2024; data Feb–Mar 2022, 95%+ of Visa Secure global transactions). Merchants completing the **3DS Method URL ≥95%** of the time see **+8% authentication success and +8% approval rate**, plus a **127% frictionless-rate lift**, vs. merchants with lower completion. Merchants populating **>50% of priority data elements** see **+4% authentication success, +6% approval, and a 57% frictionless-rate lift**. With priority data present, issuers see up to a **65% fraud-detection-rate lift** (Aug 2021 fraud data). Priority elements: browser IP, screen height/width, billing address (line 1, city, state, postal code, country), shipping address fields, cardholder email, name, phone, and the common device identification parameter. [PRIMARY] — [Visa, Global Better Data Best Practices Guide for Visa Secure with EMV 3DS](https://corporate.visa.com/content/dam/VCOM/corporate/solutions/documents/global-better-data-best-practices-guide-for-visa-secure-with-emv.pdf)
- Visa IDX via Visa Secure (formerly Visa Data Only) feeds enhanced data into Visa Advanced Authorization (VAA) risk scoring used by issuers. Visa launched the **Digital Commerce Authentication Program (DCAP)** for merchants sending four key fields (**device ID, billing address, IP address, email**) over approved Visa rails. [PRIMARY] — [Visa–Adyen case study (2026)](https://www.visa.com/vcom-assets/content/dam/visa/reimagine-visa/business/programs/documents/visa.-adyen-case-study.pdf); [Visa DCAP page](https://www.visa.com/en-us/business/programs/dcap)
- A Visa-sourced US issuer case shows a **+21 bps approval-rate** gain after adopting IDX. Visa marketing claims ">10% increase in approval rates" based on authenticated vs. unauthenticated PAN comparisons, a different baseline. [PRIMARY, marketing] — [Visa/Cardinal, A U.S. issuer's experience with Visa's IDX](https://images.win.cardinalcommerce.com/Web/VisaGlobalMarketing/%7B6689ef90-3d07-4f2a-aa68-01f097044c4a%7D_A_U.S._Issuer's_Experience_with_Visa's_IDX_Solution.pdf); [Visa Protect Merchant](https://globalclient.visa.com/Protect_Merchant)
- Broadcom/Arcot reported **+19% 3DS authentication success** over 9 months and 6M transactions with Data-Only. [VENDOR] — [Broadcom, Boosting authorization rates with 3DS Data-Only](https://arcot.broadcom.com/hubfs/Broadcom%20PaySec/Resources/Boosting%20Authorization%20Rates%20with%203-D%20Secure%20Data-Only.pdf)
- Mastercard 3RI (Identity Check Express) has no public uplift study. One provider claims US 3RI authentication success of ~60–70% and that successful 3RI performs similarly to challenged 3DS on authorization (unsourced). [VENDOR] — [Evervault 3DS high-risk Q&A](https://evervault.com/blog/3d-secure-for-high-risk-payments-webinar-q-and-a); [Acquired.com, Mastercard 3DS enhanced data standards](https://www.acquired.com/blog/mastercard-3ds-enhanced-data-standards-approval-rates/)

**Network tokens**
- The most-cited figures are a Visa CNP token auth uplift of ~**4.6%** and Mastercard ~**2.1%**, with variants of 4.3%/4.7%/3%. Fraud reduction is cited at ~28–40% for Visa tokens. A Visa circular cited by Checkout.com reports **~50% fraud reduction when 3DS and network tokenization are combined** (Europe Secure Credential Framework). Primary network documents were not retrieved, so baselines and periods are unverified. [VENDOR/PSP citing networks] — [Checkout.com, Visa Secure Credential Framework update](https://www.checkout.com/merchant-communications/visa-is-updating-its-secure-credential-framework-in-europe); [Solidgate](https://solidgate.com/blog/network-tokenization-authorization-rates/); [PCI Proxy](https://www.pci-proxy.com/blog-posts/how-network-tokenization-helps-improve-payment-authorization-rates)
- Counterpoint: Evervault reports only a **30–50 bps** auth uplift for merchants with few repeat customers. The benefit concentrates in card-on-file and repeat traffic, mostly by reducing soft declines and lifecycle (expired/reissued card) failures. [VENDOR] — [Evervault network tokens webinar Q&A](https://evervault.com/blog/evaluating-network-tokens-webinar-q-and-a)
- In the Visa/Adyen pilot, network-token transactions with enhanced data saw **+170 bps** approval vs. control. [PRIMARY] — [Visa–Adyen case study](https://www.visa.com/vcom-assets/content/dam/visa/reimagine-visa/business/programs/documents/visa.-adyen-case-study.pdf)
- 72% of merchants use one or more forms of payment tokenization (MRC 2026). [SECONDARY citing MRC] — [MRC 2026 report press release](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/merchant-risk-council-releases-2026-global-ecommerce-payments-fraud-report)

**Retry discipline and merchant advice codes** (vendor sources; verify against network rules)
- Visa caps reattempts at **15 within 30 days** for retryable declines and allows **no** retries on Category 1 codes (e.g., lost, stolen, closed). Excessive reattempts incur per-transaction fees (amounts conflict across sources). Mastercard prohibits retries after **MAC 03/21** (do not try again / cancelled recurring) under its Transaction Processing Excellence program, with per-retry fees reported at ~$0.03–$0.50. MACs 24–30 indicate specific retry windows. [VENDOR; conflicting details] — [Slicker, Visa & Mastercard retry rules (Jun 2026)](https://www.slickerhq.com/resources/blog/visa-mastercard-payment-retry-rules); [Slicker, Merchant advice codes (Oct 2026)](https://www.slickerhq.com/resources/blog/merchant-advice-codes-payment-recovery)

**Issuer-merchant data sharing / dispute deflection that also protects ratios**
- Under VAMP, disputes resolved through **RDR, CDRN, Ethoca, Verifi** in the same month are excluded from TC15 counts. TC40s are excluded only for confirmed **Visa Compelling Evidence 3.0** transactions (plus certain same-month resolutions per Checkout). Pre-dispute inquiries resolved through **Order Insight** or CE3.0 are excluded. [PSP/VENDOR] — [Checkout.com](https://www.checkout.com/docs/business-operations/manage-disputes/scheme-monitoring-programs.md); [Chargebacks911](https://chargebacks911.com/visa-acquirer-monitoring-program/)
- PayPal attributes post-FPA issuer-decline reductions "most likely" to an improved merchant risk profile in issuer models, which use 30/60/90-day rolling windows. Cutting fraud can lift issuer approvals with a lag. [PSP] — [PayPal FPA article](https://www.paypal.com/us/brc/article/auth-rate-increases-fraud-protection-advanced)

### Inferences
- Data levers are positive-sum: Visa reports simultaneous approval lift and fraud-detection lift. They should be prioritized before tuning fraud thresholds. Practical order: full 3DS Method URL + priority data → DCAP/IDX data-only for non-SCA flows → network tokens with cryptograms for card-on-file → correct CIT/MIT and stored-credential flags → MAC-compliant retry logic → Order Insight/CE3.0/RDR for dispute deflection.
- Issuer models learn a merchant's fraud profile over rolling windows. Fraud reduction and auth-rate improvement therefore reinforce each other over a 1–3-month lag. Short pre/post tests understate this.

### Gaps
- No primary Visa or Mastercard document with current network-token uplift and fraud-reduction figures was retrieved. Treat the token figures as unverified.
- No quantified evidence was found on descriptor clarity (merchant name/phone in descriptor) reducing friendly fraud. It is a widely recommended practice but uncited here.
- Stored-credential framework / MIT-CIT flagging impact on approvals was not quantified in sources found this session.
- No primary Mastercard Identity Check Express / 3RI performance data was found.

## 6. Operating model, monitoring vs. network thresholds, and high-risk vertical playbooks

### Takeaway
Leading merchants put auth rate and fraud/chargeback rate under a shared P&L objective, net approved good revenue minus total fraud cost, with one written metric dictionary and joint guardrails. Network thresholds tightened in 2025–26: Visa VAMP merchant "Excessive" fell to 1.5% (150 bps) in AP/EU/NA from 1 Apr 2026, with enforcement fees since Oct 2025. Mastercard EFM requires 3DS coverage. Merchants and PSPs therefore monitor against internal buffers well below these lines. High-risk verticals face higher chargeback baselines and network risk-program scrutiny. Public, reliable benchmarks for them are thin.

### Cited Findings
**Network thresholds (2025–2026)**
- **Visa VAMP** (effective April 2025, replacing VFMP and VDMP). Ratio = (TC15 + TC40, minus exceptions) ÷ total settled transactions. Minimum 1,500 combined TC15 + TC40 per month. Merchant thresholds from 1 Jun 2025: LAC 150 bps; AP/EU/NA 220 bps; CEMEA 220 bps. **From 1 Apr 2026: LAC 150 bps; AP/EU/NA 150 bps; CEMEA 220 bps.** Acquirer portfolio: above-standard 50–70 bps, excessive ≥70 bps. Enumeration: ≥20% with ≥300k enumerated transactions. [PSP] — [Checkout.com](https://www.checkout.com/docs/business-operations/manage-disputes/scheme-monitoring-programs.md); [Chargebacks911 VAMP](https://chargebacks911.com/visa-acquirer-monitoring-program/)
- VAMP fees: **$8 per TC40/TC15** for Excessive merchants and acquirers; $4 for above-standard acquirers. First violation in a rolling 12 months gets a 3-month grace period. Acquirers may then face fines on every dispute/TC40 for any merchant above **0.3%**. Remediation plans are due within 15 days. Enforcement fees began **1 Oct 2025**. Visa moved to "risk-based" enforcement and introduced the OneERS dashboard. [VENDOR] — [Chargebacks911](https://chargebacks911.com/visa-acquirer-monitoring-program/). Ravelin notes most merchants felt VAMP impact from January 2026. [VENDOR] — [Ravelin 2026](https://www.ravelin.com/blog/3d-secure-rates-2026.md)
- Regional conflict: some sources place LAC at 1.5% and CEMEA at 2.2%. Another says the changes apply everywhere except Latin America. [VENDOR conflict] — [Redo VAMP 2026](https://redo.com/resources/articles/chargebacks/vamp-2026-shopify); [Chargebacks911](https://chargebacks911.com/visa-acquirer-monitoring-program/)
- The 0.3% acquirer-level fine trigger means acquirers push merchants well below 1.5%. Acquirer portfolio thresholds of 0.5%/0.7% also lead acquirers to impose tighter contractual limits on individual merchants. [VENDOR] — [Chargebacks911](https://chargebacks911.com/visa-acquirer-monitoring-program/); [Checkout.com](https://www.checkout.com/docs/business-operations/manage-disputes/scheme-monitoring-programs.md)
- **Mastercard EFM**: see Section 2 (≥1,000 sales, ≥$50k fraud chargebacks, ≥0.5% fraud chargeback-to-sales, low 3DS share; fines up to $100k/month). **Cartes Bancaires**: fraud program at >0.50% fraud-to-sales and >€20k for four consecutive months; disputes program at >2% and >200 disputes. [PSP] — [Checkout.com](https://www.checkout.com/docs/business-operations/manage-disputes/scheme-monitoring-programs.md)

**Ownership and KPIs**
- Definitions drift between payments and finance (first-attempt vs. eventual success). Fraud controls directly depress auth rate, and a fraud tool can cut chargebacks while silently raising false declines. These sources recommend one precisely defined shared auth KPI, a named owner, and false-decline metrics tracked alongside. Evidence is vendor-level. [VENDOR] — [Primer KPI guide](https://primer.io/blog/payment-metrics-and-kpis); [Spreedly, establishing auth-rate ownership](https://www.spreedly.com/blog/5-steps-to-establishing-effective-auth-rate-ownership); [PayPal, setting and tracking fraud KPIs](https://pep.paypal.com/us/brc/article/setting-and-tracking-fraud-kpis); [Ixopay](https://www.ixopay.com/payment-knowledge-hub/successful-authorization-rate-kpi)
- MRC 2026 Global eCommerce Payments & Fraud Report (released 18 Mar 2026 with Visa Acceptance Solutions and Verifi; 1,278 merchant professionals, 37 countries): **64%** report rising first-party misuse, a quarter of them by ≥25%. Average **3.8 fraud attack types** per merchant (a Substack analysis says 3.7). **72%** use tokenization. 80%+ cite technology infrastructure as their biggest fraud challenge. 43% accept real-time payments, and 63% are exploring agentic AI payments. [PRIMARY survey via press release] — [MRC 2026 press release (NatLawReview)](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/merchant-risk-council-releases-2026-global-ecommerce-payments-fraud-report); [Visa Acceptance Solutions report page](https://www.visaacceptance.com/en-us/insights/fraud-report.html); [Substack analysis](https://substack.com/home/post/p-191927921)
- Chargeback windows are long, so lagging indicators (chargebacks) must be supplemented with leading ones: TC40s (Uber), decline-code mix, enumeration ratio, approval-rate drops. [PRIMARY merchant/acquirer] — [Uber Engineering](https://www.uber.com/blog/how-uber-processes-early-chargeback-signals); [J.P. Morgan card testing paper](https://JPMorgan.com/content/dam/jpm/securities/documents/card-testing-white-paper-ada-final.pdf)

**High-risk verticals**
- Vendor-compiled chargeback benchmarks (2025–26, not a single unified dataset): standard e-commerce ~0.25–0.45%; SaaS/subscriptions 0.65–1.20%; digital goods 0.66–1.50%; travel & hospitality 0.89–2.00%; adult 1.10–2.60%. Friendly fraud is cited at 60–80% of chargebacks in digital goods/subscriptions. [VENDOR, low reliability] — [EightX, average chargeback rate by vertical](https://eightx.co/blog/average-chargeback-rate-by-vertical); [Chargeback Help, vertical benchmarking](https://chargebackhelp.com/?p=987188)
- Visa's Integrity Risk Program tiers high-risk MCCs including 7995 (gambling), 5967, 7273 and 6051 (quasi-cash/crypto; some sources conflict on 6051 descriptions). Digital games are commonly MCC 5816. [VENDOR] — [Basis Theory, high-risk MCCs](https://blog.basistheory.com/high-risk-mccs); [PaymentCloud high-risk MCC codes](https://paymentcloudinc.com/blog/high-risk-mcc-codes/)

### Inferences
- **Buffering:** with Visa's merchant line at 150 bps (AP/EU/NA), the acquirer fine trigger at 30 bps per merchant, and acquirer portfolio limits at 50–70 bps, the practical internal target for most merchants is to keep the VAMP ratio comfortably below ~30–50 bps. Escalate at a fraction of that. Because 1,500 TC40+TC15 is the minimum count, low-volume merchants are shielded from the merchant ratio but not from acquirer pressure. (Inference; no single source states a recommended buffer.)
- **Count-based VAMP + rising first-party misuse** means dispute deflection (RDR, CDRN/Ethoca, Order Insight, CE3.0) is now a ratio-management tool, not just a cost tool. Clear descriptors and fast refunds/cancellations reduce non-fraud TC15s.
- **Operating model:** a joint payments + risk forum (weekly) owning one objective function, net margin after fraud, with guardrails (auth rate, VAMP/EFM ratios with buffers, TRA fraud rate for EU acquirers, enumeration ratio). Daily/real-time monitors cover approval-rate drops, decline-code mix, card-testing spikes, and TC40 inflow. Monthly reviews cover matured chargebacks and threshold re-tuning.
- **High-risk vertical playbook** (digital goods, gaming, travel, crypto/6051): segment thresholds by ticket size and customer tenure. Use 3DS/step-up for new customers and high-ticket orders. Use tokens and stored credentials for returning users. Aggressively deflect friendly fraud. Monitor enumeration (digital goods are a prime card-testing target). Consider APM routing (A2A, wallets) for high-risk segments to reduce card exposure. Expect higher issuer decline baselines for MCCs like 6051 that issuers treat as quasi-cash.

### Gaps
- Mastercard ECM/HECM thresholds were not retrieved from a primary or PSP source in this session. Verify current values with Mastercard or an acquirer.
- Whether Visa counts both a TC40 and a TC15 on the same fraudulent transaction (double-counting) was not verified.
- No authoritative 2025–26 decline-rate benchmarks by vertical (including MCC 6051 crypto purchases) were found. Available numbers are vendor illustrations.
- No primary research comparing organizational models (centralized vs. split payments/risk ownership) was found.

## 7. Published case studies with numbers (auth up AND fraud/chargebacks flat or down)

### Takeaway
Very few public case studies report both a measured auth-rate increase and a measured fraud/chargeback decrease with a control group. The strongest recent example is the Visa/Adyen IDX pilot (2025–26): +170–310 bps first-attempt approvals with "stable" fraud. Most other cases are vendor anecdotes or uncontrolled pre/post analyses.

### Cited Findings
- **Visa × Adyen, enterprise travel merchant (Visa, 2026):** Visa IDX via Visa Secure (formerly Data Only) fed enhanced data into VAA, with no consumer friction added. Results vs. standard non-authenticated e-commerce (ECI 07): **+290 bps cross-border, +190 bps US domestic, +310 bps PAN, +170 bps network tokens; fraud rates remained stable.** The lift was strongest on transactions previously declined as suspected fraud. Period: 8 Oct 2025 – 22 Jan 2026. Caveats: **subset of optimized US issuers only**; excludes wallets, standard 3DS, MIT and AFTs. [PRIMARY network + PSP] — [Visa–Adyen case study](https://www.visa.com/vcom-assets/content/dam/visa/reimagine-visa/business/programs/documents/visa.-adyen-case-study.pdf)

No magnitudes and no control group. [PSP] — [PayPal](https://www.paypal.com/us/brc/article/auth-rate-increases-fraud-protection-advanced)
- **Primer Adaptive 3DS (MPE 2024 handbook):** one merchant reports a **+2% authorization rate** using adaptive 3DS (no fraud figure). The same handbook describes a global merchant service provider cutting chargebacks **from 5% to below 1%** (tools unnamed). [VENDOR, 2024] — [MPE 2024 Payments Challenges Handbook case studies](https://www.merchantpaymentsecosystem.com/files/documents/mpe-2024-payments-challenges-handbook-sponsor-case-studies.pdf)

[VENDOR] — [MPE 2025 sponsor case studies](https://www.merchantpaymentsecosystem.com/files/documents/mpe-2025-sponsor-case-studies.pdf)

This is a cross-sectional cohort comparison, not a single-merchant case. [PRIMARY] — [Visa Better Data guide](https://corporate.visa.com/content/dam/VCOM/corporate/solutions/documents/global-better-data-best-practices-guide-for-visa-secure-with-emv.pdf)

### Inferences
- The best-supported "both metrics improve" mechanism is better data to issuers (IDX/Data Only, 3DS priority data, tokens). Threshold tuning or vendor switching rarely has controlled public evidence.
- When citing vendor case studies, the report should flag the selection bias: success stories are published, failures are not. Missing controls, short windows, or "subset of optimized issuers" scoping inflate the apparent effect.

### Gaps
- No public merchant engineering case study (e.g., Uber, Airbnb, Netflix, Booking, Shopify) with paired auth-rate and chargeback-rate numbers was found.
- No neutral academic or regulator study quantifying the joint auth/fraud effect of any single vendor tool was found.
