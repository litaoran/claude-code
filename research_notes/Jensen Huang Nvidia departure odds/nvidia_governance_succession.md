## 1. Board power and mechanics to remove Huang (who sits on the board, lead director, investigation committee, bylaws, Huang's voting stake)

### Takeaway
Removing Huang as CEO is legally simple: under Nvidia's bylaws every officer serves "at the pleasure of the Board" and can be removed "at any time" by board vote, any two directors can call a special meeting on 24 hours' notice, and Huang is 1 of 11 directors with only ~3.6% of a single-class share base, so he cannot block it. The practical constraint is board composition: a long-tenured, founder-era board with an independent Lead Director (Stephen Neal, 77) rather than an independent chair, no current/former large-cap CEOs, and no disclosed CEO succession plan. Removing him as a *director* would require a stockholder vote (supermajority without cause under the 2024 bylaws).

### Cited Findings
**Removal mechanics (bylaws, amended and restated March 12, 2024)**
- Officers: "All officers shall hold office at the pleasure of the Board of Directors and until their successors shall have been duly elected and qualified, unless sooner removed. Any officer elected or appointed by the Board of Directors may be removed at any time by the Board of Directors. If the office of any officer becomes vacant for any reason, the vacancy may be filled by the Board of Directors." (Section 29(a)) — [NVIDIA Bylaws, Ex. 3.1 to 8-K filed 3/14/2024](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581024000069/exhibit31-amendedandrestat.htm)
- Special board meetings may be called "by the Chairman of the Board, the Chief Executive Officer, the President, any two directors…"; notice of special meetings is due "at least twenty-four (24) hours before the date and time of the meeting" (oral/electronic notice) (Section 22(b), (d)). — [Bylaws](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581024000069/exhibit31-amendedandrestat.htm)
- Director removal: directors "may be removed from office at any time (i) with cause by the affirmative vote of the holders of a majority of the voting power of all the then-outstanding shares… or (ii) without cause by the affirmative vote of the holders of sixty-six and two-thirds percent (66-2/3%)…" (Section 21). — [Bylaws](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581024000069/exhibit31-amendedandrestat.htm)

### Inferences
- Huang has no structural defense (no dual-class stock, no chair role, ~3.6% stake, one board vote). A majority of the 10 independent directors can remove him as CEO at a special meeting called by any two directors. He could remain a director unless he resigns or stockholders remove him (majority of outstanding shares with cause; 66-2/3% without cause under the 2024 bylaws).
- My tenure math for the 10 2026 nominees: mean ~18 years including Huang (~16 years excluding him), mean age ~66. Semafor's "almost 15 years" probably uses a different method (for example, counting Nora Johnson). Either way, three of 11 seats (Huang, Coxe, Jones) date to 1993. Coxe and Stevens come from Sutter Hill and Sequoia, Nvidia's early VC backers. That makes the "captured board" critique plausible. But it also means these directors hold a lot of stock (Coxe ~30.6M shares, Stevens ~34.1M shares per the proxy), so the stock drop hurts them directly.
- The board's move from "confidence" (Nov 8) to reportedly preparing an ouster (Nov 11) came right after the Powell report. That fits the pattern where a relationship with a subordinate, rather than the infidelity itself, triggers board action (see Section 3).

### Gaps
- Members of the independent investigation committee are not public. I could not confirm whether Neal chairs it.
- Whether the bylaws or charter supermajority provisions were amended after the 2026 advisory vote. The bylaws quoted are the March 2024 version.
- Whether the board met on Nov 11–12 and what it decided. No 8-K Item 5.02 had been filed as of my EDGAR check (latest Nvidia 8-K found: Sept 3, 2026).
- Huang's employment terms: I did not find an employment agreement or severance agreement for him in the proxy excerpts reviewed. Only plan-based change-in-control acceleration is disclosed.

## 2. Nvidia Code of Conduct and policies relevant to the reported Powell relationship

### Takeaway
Nvidia's public Code of Conduct (© 2023; PDF last modified Sept 2024) does not contain an explicit ban on romantic or manager–subordinate relationships. I found no "romantic" language in it. It frames "personal relationships" as a type of activity that "may create a conflict of interest." It requires people to avoid actual or potential conflicts, step out of related decisions, and seek guidance from Employment Legal. It points to a separate, non-public Conflict of Interest Policy. Any waiver for an executive officer needs Compliance Committee approval and must be disclosed. So the likely breach theory is an undisclosed conflict of interest by a CEO with a direct report (pay and promotion decisions), not a per-se dating ban like Intel's or McDonald's.

### Cited Findings
- "We avoid conflicts of interest. › We make decisions in the best interests of NVIDIA. › We avoid situations where our actions might create a conflict—actual or potential—between our personal gain and our obligations to NVIDIA. If such conflicts arise, we immediately remove ourselves from any decision-making role in the matter." — [NVIDIA Code of Conduct (external PDF)](https://www.nvidia.com/content/dam/en-zz/Solutions/about-us/documents/NVIDIA-Code-of-Conduct-external.pdf)
- "The following types of activities may create a conflict of interest:" the list includes "Advisory roles or board seat participation," "Outside employment or consulting," "Self-employment or volunteering," and "Investments or personal relationships." It also says: "If involved in a situation that could present a perceived or actual conflict of interest, contact Employment Legal for guidance." Key policies referenced: "Conflict of Interest Policy" and "Outside Activities-Conflict of Interest Preapproval Process." — [NVIDIA Code of Conduct](https://www.nvidia.com/content/dam/en-zz/Solutions/about-us/documents/NVIDIA-Code-of-Conduct-external.pdf)
- "Managers serve as role models. › Managers at NVIDIA play a special role, promoting equitable and ethical decisions, and fostering safe working environments." — [NVIDIA Code of Conduct](https://www.nvidia.com/content/dam/en-zz/Solutions/about-us/documents/NVIDIA-Code-of-Conduct-external.pdf)
- "NVIDIA's Board of Directors has formed a Compliance Committee to ensure ethics concerns are addressed appropriately." "Any waiver of Our Code requires written approval from the Compliance Committee. If the waiver involves a member of the board of directors or an executive officer, it will be disclosed in line with applicable rules or laws." — [NVIDIA Code of Conduct](https://www.nvidia.com/content/dam/en-zz/Solutions/about-us/documents/NVIDIA-Code-of-Conduct-external.pdf)
- An older Nvidia code (undated Scribd copy) lists "personnel decisions regarding fellow employees with whom we have a personal relationship" as a potential conflict. This is secondary, and it may not be the current version. — summarized via search result for [Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/397406986/t-table)
- Reporting: Powell "reports to" Huang. NYT cited five former or current employees. Nvidia, Huang and Powell had not confirmed the claims. — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/nvidia-board-reportedly-preparing-oust-233419551.html); [OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/nvidias-board-working-with-outside-firm-to-replace-jensen-huang-could-name-temporary-chief-bloomberg/)
- Powell joined Nvidia in 2008 and has led healthcare since 2017. NYT reported Huang and Powell were captured on camera being affectionate at a San Jose hotel "in March." A former employee said rumors had circulated for years. Huang reportedly hired crisis PR firm Heller "as early as last year." — [CNBC, Nov 11](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/11/nvidia-jensen-huang-affair-employee.html)
- Semafor: the company "faces questions about an earlier internal investigation into the allegations." Former SEC chair Gary Gensler: "It appears [Huang] did mislead the public." — [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/09/2026/with-a-weak-board-and-no-successor-nvidia-confronts-a-governance-crisis)

### Inferences
- With no explicit fraternization ban, the board's grounds would likely rest on (a) failure to disclose a conflict involving a direct report, (b) whether Huang influenced Powell's pay or promotions, (c) candor toward the board or the earlier internal probe, and (d) the national-security dimension. These are the same "lack of candor" grounds used at BP and Nestlé (Section 3).
- The 2026 proxy shows that Huang's recommendations feed into NEO pay ("Mr. Huang's recommendations for other NEOs' compensation") and that "Our CEO also selects senior leaders to engage directly with the Board." That makes CEO influence over subordinates' careers a live issue. Powell is not an NEO, so her pay would not appear in the proxy.

### Gaps
- The text of Nvidia's internal Conflict of Interest Policy and any rule on manager–subordinate relationships (not public).
- Whether Huang disclosed the Powell relationship to Compliance or the board, and what the "earlier internal investigation" covered.

## 3. Precedents: boards ousting CEOs over undisclosed relationships and conflicts (timeline from revelation to exit)

### Takeaway
Recent precedents show boards act fast once an undisclosed relationship with a subordinate, or a lack of candor about one, is confirmed: often within days (McDonald's, BP) or within weeks to months if a first probe is inconclusive (Nestlé). The outgoing CEO is usually replaced immediately by a CFO or a board chair as interim (Intel, BP, Kohl's) or by a ready internal successor (McDonald's, Nestlé). Shareholder outcomes depended on whether a successor was ready.

### Cited Findings
- **Intel / Brian Krzanich (2018):** resigned June 21, 2018 over a past consensual relationship with an employee that breached a policy for managers. CFO Bob Swan was interim for about 7 months and named permanent CEO in January 2019. Stock fell 2.4% on day one, and one-year total return was −8.7% vs S&P +8.5%. "Intel had no successor ready." — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/nvidia-board-reportedly-preparing-oust-233419551.html)
- **McDonald's / Steve Easterbrook (2019):** fired Nov 3, 2019 over a consensual relationship with an employee that violated policy. "The board named Chris Kempczinski, who ran the U.S. business, as CEO in the same announcement." Stock fell 2.7% the next trading day, and one-year total return was +14.7% vs S&P +11.9%. McDonald's sued Easterbrook in August 2020 for allegedly hiding other relationships, and in 2021 he agreed to return over $105M. — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/nvidia-board-reportedly-preparing-oust-233419551.html)
- **BP / Bernard Looney (2023):** resigned with immediate effect Sept 12, 2023 (under 4 years as CEO) for failing to fully disclose past relationships with colleagues. A May 2022 board review of earlier allegations had found no code breach, and a later batch of allegations prompted a new investigation. CFO Murray Auchincloss became interim CEO. — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2023/9/13/bp-chief-bernard-looney-steps-down-over-past-relationships-with-colleagues); [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2023/09/12/bps-irish-chief-executive-bernard-looney-to-resign)
- **Nestlé / Laurent Freixe (2025):** dismissed effective immediately in early September 2025 after an investigation into an undisclosed romantic relationship with a direct subordinate, which breached the code of conduct. Philipp Navratil replaced him, and Freixe got no exit package. Per Reuters, concerns first surfaced in the spring via an internal hotline. The first probe was inconclusive, and a second probe with outside counsel confirmed the relationship; Freixe had initially denied it to the board. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/europe/nestle-dismisses-ceo-investigation-relationship-subordinate-rcna228471); [KSL/Reuters](https://www.ksl.com/article/news/business/nestle-abruptly-removes-ceo-freixe-over-undisclosed-relationship-with-subordinate/51368613)
- **Kohl's / Ashley Buchanan (2025):** terminated "for cause" in May 2025, a little over 100 days after starting Jan 15, after an outside-counsel probe found he steered business to a vendor founded by someone with whom he had a personal relationship, on favorable terms, and caused a multimillion-dollar consulting agreement involving the same person. He forfeited all equity, including the $2.5M signing bonus, and was removed from the board. Board chair Michael Bender became interim CEO. Shares rose as much as 8%. — [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/markets/kohls-fires-ceo-after-investigation.amp); [ABC7](https://abc7news.com/post/kohls-fired-brand-new-ceo-unethical-behavior/16295047/)
- Gil Luria (D.A. Davidson) on Nvidia: "Mr. Huang being removed immediately could be challenging for Nvidia since it does not have a succession plan in place." Baird analysts favor an outside hire, and Dan Ives estimates Nvidia would be worth 25–30% less without Huang (analyst opinions). A Blind survey found 88% of 232 Nvidia employees want Huang to stay. — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/nvidia-board-reportedly-preparing-oust-233419551.html)
- John C. Coffee Jr. (Columbia): Huang's case goes beyond "the usual sex scandal in the corporate boardroom," and "negligence doesn't require that you shoot yourself in the foot by firing a well regarded CEO." — [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/09/2026/with-a-weak-board-and-no-successor-nvidia-confronts-a-governance-crisis)

### Inferences
- In every precedent the decisive facts were the subordinate relationship and non-disclosure or lack of candor, not infidelity as such. The Nov 11 Powell report (an alleged direct report) moves Nvidia's facts closer to the Krzanich, Easterbrook and Freixe pattern than the Nov 8 facts were.
- No precedent involves a founder-CEO of Huang's tenure (33 years) or a company this dependent on one leader. The closest structural analog is Intel (no ready successor, CFO as interim), which produced the worst shareholder outcome in the Yahoo comparison.

### Gaps
- Exact revelation-to-exit day counts for Intel and Kohl's (start dates of the probes were not in the sources fetched).
- Later BP clawback outcomes and the SEC action against Easterbrook were not verified in this session.

## 4. Interim CEO and successor candidates; heir apparent; executive changes 2024–2026

### Takeaway
There is no named heir apparent, no President or COO, and no disclosed CEO succession plan. Huang has publicly rejected formal succession planning. Names floated for interim CEO are CFO Colette Kress (58) and Lead Director Stephen Neal (77). For permanent CEO, the names floated are mostly outsiders (Lisa Su, Hock Tan, Rene Haas, Pat Gelsinger and others) plus insider Ian Buck and family members. All of these come from speculation by commentators and governance experts, not from board leaks. Recent senior changes (Jay Puri retiring; Microsoft's Nick Parker hired as EVP; Intel's CAO hired) look like bench refresh rather than a successor being groomed.

## 7. Compensation structure and retention hooks

### Takeaway
Huang's pay is modest relative to his wealth: FY2026 total $36.3M, all equity in PSUs. His unvested awards were worth about $265M at the FY2026 year-end, a rounding error next to a roughly $150B+ stake. That gives the board almost no compensation leverage, and it gives Huang no meaningful financial reason to stay or go. Vesting runs on 4-year (SY PSU) and 3-year (MY PSU) schedules, with the FY2026 MY PSUs to be certified by March 2028.

### Cited Findings
- Summary Compensation Table: FY2026 salary $1,497,627; stock awards $24,800,511; non-equity incentive $6,000,000; other $4,045,691; **total $36,343,830** (FY2025 $49,866,251; FY2024 $34,167,902). — [2026 DEF 14A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000036/nvda-20260512.htm)

FY2026 MY PSUs to be certified "by March 2028." — [2026 DEF 14A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000036/nvda-20260512.htm). ### Inferences
- If terminated "for cause" (the Kohl's precedent), Huang would forfeit unvested PSUs, roughly $0.3B, which is immaterial to him. Clawback or forfeiture is therefore not a meaningful lever either way.
- The large FY2027 cash-target increases for the four other NEOs (to 150% of salary) look like a retention move for the senior bench, adopted before the scandal.
